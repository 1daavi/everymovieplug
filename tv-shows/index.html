A short guide

How to choose a series

  1. 1

    Do the math on the commitment.

    Eight episodes of fifty minutes is roughly seven hours. A long-running series with several seasons can be dozens of hours. Know the size of the commitment before you start.

  2. 2

    Check whether it is finished.

    A limited series tells one story and ends. An ongoing series may be renewed, cancelled or still airing. If you hate unresolved endings, look up the status first.

  3. 3

    Decide between a binge and a weekly watch.

    Some services release a whole season at once, and others release an episode a week. Weekly shows are great for conversation and spoilers are everywhere, so plan accordingly.

  4. 4

    Use the three-episode rule.

    Pilots are hard to write, and many great series find their footing by episode two or three. Give a show three episodes unless it is plainly not for you.

  5. 5

    Look at where it streams.

    Catalogs vary by country and change often. Open the title page to see which service has the show in your region and whether it is included in a subscription.

Know what you are looking at

Ratings, explained

Scores for TV work a little differently from movies, because a series changes from season to season.

TMDB user score

The average of ratings from registered TMDB members, out of 10, for the series as a whole. A long show's score blends strong and weak seasons together.

IMDb rating

A weighted average of user votes from 1 to 10. IMDb also rates individual episodes, which helps you spot a dip, or a standout episode, inside a season.

Rotten Tomatoes

Critic scores are usually given per season. The Tomatometer is the percentage of positive reviews rather than an average grade, so a high percentage means broad approval, not perfection.

Metacritic

Scores a season on a 0 to 100 scale by weighting critic reviews. It is useful for seeing whether critics think a show is improving or fading.

Viewership numbers

Networks and streamers sometimes publish viewership. Treat the figures carefully, because each company counts differently. For example, in our report on Lanterns the finale set a series record, yet a second season was still undecided.

Questions

Frequently asked

How are the trending TV shows chosen?+

The weekly list comes from TMDB's trending chart, which is based on activity on TMDB. It shows what people are looking up and rating right now, not total viewing hours.

What is the difference between a season finale and a series finale?+

A season finale ends a season and may leave the story open for more. A series finale ends the whole show. A show can have a season finale and still be waiting on a renewal decision.

Can I watch shows on Every Movie Plug?+

No. We do not host or stream anything. Title pages show where a series is legally available using streaming data from JustWatch.

Why do some TV genres look different from movie genres?+

TMDB groups some TV categories together, such as action with adventure and sci-fi with fantasy, and has no separate horror, romance or thriller category for TV. The mood filters are a handy way around that.

Email contact@everymovieplug.com or message us on X at @everymovieplug. We read everything and correct errors we confirm.
