Avengers: Endgame Retakes the Box-Office Crown From Avatar

By Maccarthy Albin

October 5, 2026

A new extended re-release has put Marvel's finale back on top of the all-time chart, by a razor-thin margin.

Avengers: Endgame is the highest-grossing movie of all time again. A new extended re-release, Avengers: Endgame Encore, added more than $126 million worldwide and pushed the film's total to about $2.9255 billion.

That puts it only slightly ahead of Avatar, which stands at $2.9237 billion after its own string of re-releases.

A Lead of Less Than $2 Million

The margin deserves attention. The gap between the two films is about $1.8 million on a total near $2.9 billion, which is a difference of less than a tenth of one percent. In practical terms, the crown rests on a figure small enough that a few more days of ticket sales for Avatar, or a change in how a market’s receipts are reported, could shift the order again.

The size of the Encore’s contribution is easier to grasp by working backward. If the re-release added more than $126 million and the new total is about $2.9255 billion, Endgame stood at roughly $2.8 billion beforehand, which is close to its original finish of around $2.797 billion plus small additions since. It was the Encore, rather than anything earlier, that carried the film across the line.

A Record That Keeps Changing Hands

Marvel's heroes and Pandora's Na'vi have traded this record before. The back-and-forth goes like this:

  • 2009 to 2019: Avatar held the top spot for nearly a decade after earning $2.789 billion in its original run.
  • July 2019: Endgame passed it during its first summer in theaters, finishing around $2.797 billion.
  • March 2021: A re-release of Avatar in China brought in roughly $58 million, enough to put it back on top at more than $2.8 billion.
  • October 2026: The Encore cut lifts Endgame past $2.925 billion and returns it to first place.

Seen over time, the pattern is striking. Avatar began at $2.789 billion and now stands at $2.9237 billion, meaning its re-releases have added roughly $135 million to its total. Endgame’s own re-release has delivered a comparable sum in a single run. The two films have effectively become competitors in a long contest decided less by new audiences than by the willingness of old fans to return.

The All-Time Leaderboard Today

Beyond the top two, the chart looks like this:

  1. Avengers: Endgame (2019): $2.9255 billion
  2. Avatar (2009): $2.9237 billion
  3. Spider-Man: Brand New Day: $2.5 billion
  4. Avatar: The Way of Water (2022): $2.34 billion
  5. Ne Zha 2 (2025): $2.27 billion

The list shows how concentrated the upper end of the chart has become. Disney-owned titles occupy three of the five places, including both Avatar films, while Spider-Man: Brand New Day and Ne Zha 2 complete the list from outside that group. The distance between the first two and the rest is also considerable, since the third-place film trails the leaders by well over $400 million.

What the Encore Cut Offered Audiences

Nostalgia was only part of it. Disney and Marvel positioned the 185-minute Encore edition as a lead-in to Avengers: Doomsday. It adds extended battle footage and a new introduction setting up the next phase of the Multiverse story.

Fans turned out in force for IMAX and Dolby Cinema screenings to see it on the biggest screens available.

The approach gave returning viewers a reason beyond familiarity. A re-release with new material is a different proposition from a straight reissue, since it invites even those who have seen the film many times to buy another ticket. Tying the edition to the upcoming Doomsday also served a marketing purpose, reminding audiences of the stakes of the story before the next chapter arrives.

Why Studios Keep Bringing Old Hits Back

The Encore is one example of a broader strategy. Re-releasing a proven film costs far less than producing a new one, and the audience already exists. Premium formats such as IMAX and Dolby Cinema add further appeal, because they offer an experience that home viewing cannot match, which helps justify a return visit to the theater.

The same logic explains why Avatar has returned to cinemas several times. Each re-release draws a new group of viewers who were too young to see the film originally, along with longtime fans who want to see it again on large screens. For a record decided by margins this narrow, even a modest re-release can change the order of the chart.

Disney Wins Either Way

Since Disney bought 21st Century Fox, both Marvel and Avatar belong to the same company. Whichever film is on top, the money goes to the same studio.

That ownership changes the character of the rivalry. Fans may treat it as a contest between two franchises, but the company behind both has little reason to prefer either outcome, and every re-release benefits the same ledger. If anything, the narrow gap creates a steady stream of headlines that keeps both properties in the public eye.

A Note on Reading the Numbers

Worldwide totals of this size are compiled from many territories and reporting sources, and they are usually expressed in rounded figures. When two films are separated by under $2 million, small differences in how receipts are tallied, converted between currencies or updated can matter. The totals quoted here are the ones reported at the time of writing, and they will keep moving as the Encore run continues and as Avatar collects further receipts.

The wider point holds regardless of the final decimal. Both films have surpassed $2.9 billion, a level no other release has approached, and their rivalry has outlasted the original theatrical runs of either one. That longevity is itself part of the story: two films released a decade apart are still competing for the same title, and both continue to draw audiences back to cinemas years after their debuts.

Will the Crown Stay Put?

With more Avatar sequels and Avengers films on the schedule, this back-and-forth probably isn't over.

A lead of under $2 million is hardly secure. Another Avatar re-release, or continued strong business for the Encore itself, could settle the question one way or the other, and the arrival of Avengers: Doomsday may bring renewed attention to its predecessor. For now, Endgame holds the title, and Marvel Studios marked the moment on social media.
