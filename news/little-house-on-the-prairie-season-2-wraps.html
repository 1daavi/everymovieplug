Little House on the Prairie Wraps Season 2 and Adds 10 Cast Members

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The second season adapts On the Banks of Plum Creek.

Filming has wrapped on Season 2 of Netflix's Little House on the Prairie, and ten new actors have joined the cast, according to What's on Netflix. Production had been expected to run into November, so it finished a few weeks early.

The announcement closes the shooting phase of the second season and moves the series into post-production, the stage in which editing, sound, music and visual effects are completed. For a drama set in the nineteenth-century American frontier, that work carries particular weight, since the look of the landscape and the period details are central to how the show is received.

Production Finishes Ahead of Schedule

Showrunner Rebecca Sonnenshine thanked the crew and the production partners CBS Studios, Anonymous Content and Netflix. The season adapts Laura Ingalls Wilder's On the Banks of Plum Creek.

Wrapping weeks ahead of the expected November finish is not always a given on a large-scale period production, where weather, locations and the demands of working with child actors can push schedules in either direction. The early completion suggests the shoot ran smoothly, and it leaves the production team additional time on the back end of the process. Netflix has not said how that extra time might affect the timing of the release.

A Season Drawn From the Banks of Plum Creek

Wilder's On the Banks of Plum Creek was published in 1937 and follows the Ingalls family as they settle in Minnesota near the town of Walnut Grove. The family's first home there is a dugout set into the bank of a creek, a detail that has become one of the book's best-known images. The story moves through the first years of a new community, with the family adjusting to a new school, a church and a farming life that depends heavily on the weather.

The novel is also notable for introducing Nellie Oleson, the proud and sharp-tongued daughter of the local storekeepers, who becomes a rival to Laura. Among its most memorable episodes is a devastating invasion of grasshoppers that destroys the family's crops and tests their finances. Reverend Alden, a minister who appears in the book, is among the characters who now have a place in the series, and the casting of Willa Dunn as Nellie Oleson places that rivalry at the center of the new season. Adapting the book allows the series to move beyond its earlier setting and into a more settled, community-oriented chapter of the family's story.

Ten Actors Join the Ensemble

The newly announced cast members and their roles are listed below. All but one are recurring; Scott Porter appears as a guest star.

  • David Wronko as Thomas Perry (recurring)
  • Erik Athavale as Reverend Alden (recurring)
  • Elyse Levesque as Emma Perry (recurring)
  • Graham Harvey as Johnny Johnson (recurring)
  • Jacinta Forest Lewitski as Hannah Walker (recurring)
  • Lyla Porter-Follows as Mrs. Lena Nelson (recurring)
  • Martin Roach as Simon Hughes (recurring)
  • Paul Greene as Luther Perry (recurring)
  • Ryan Northcott as William Oleson (recurring)
  • Scott Porter as Peter Ingalls (guest star)

The size of the group reflects the nature of the source material. A story set in a growing town needs neighbors, shopkeepers, clergy and rival families, and the roster of recurring roles suggests the series intends to build out Walnut Grove as a full community rather than keeping the focus on a single household. Three characters share the surname Perry, which points to at least one family group among the new arrivals, while the Oleson name returns through William Oleson alongside the previously announced Nellie and Margaret.

Familiar Faces Return

Willa Dunn (Nellie Oleson), Charlotte Sullivan (Margaret Oleson) and Rachelle Lefevre (Eva Beadle) were announced earlier. Returning from Season 1 are Alice Halsey as Laura Ingalls, Luke Bracey as Charles Ingalls, Crosby Fitzgerald as Caroline Ingalls, Skywalker Hughes as Mary Ingalls and Warren Christie as John Edwards.

Keeping the five core performers in place gives the second season continuity at its center. The Ingalls family remains the anchor of the series, and the additional cast members are positioned around them. Peter Ingalls, played by guest star Scott Porter, carries the family surname and adds a further connection to the household at the heart of the story.

Waiting on a Premiere Date

Netflix has not announced a premiere date. What's on Netflix estimates a summer 2027 release, but that is its own projection, not an official date. Based on the episode listings, the season appears to run eight episodes.

Until the streamer confirms a schedule, viewers should treat the summer 2027 window as a forecast rather than a commitment. Netflix typically releases a first trailer and a date announcement closer to launch, and with filming complete, the next developments to watch for are casting images, a teaser and a formal release date. The fact that production finished early gives the studio flexibility, but the timeline now depends on how long post-production takes.
