Street Fighter Reviews Are Mixed as Critics Split on the Fights, the Cast and the Tone

Rotten Tomatoes sits at 38% and Metacritic in the mid-40s, with critics praising the leads and the 2D animation but disagreeing on the writing and tone.

The first reviews of the live-action Street Fighter are in, and they are mixed. The film, directed by Kitao Sakurai (Twisted Metal), had its world premiere in Los Angeles on October 12, and the review embargo lifted on Thursday. It opens in theaters on Friday, October 16. For how it performed once it arrived, see our report on its opening weekend.

The score. Capcom's adaptation was at 38% on Rotten Tomatoes on Thursday, according to MovieWeb, after debuting at 34%. Forbes says that score was based on 32 reviews, while MovieWeb counts 34, and both note it will move as more reviews post. On Metacritic the film sits in the mid-40s, with World of Reel reporting 45 and MovieWeb 46. Before the embargo lifted, traders on Kalshi had forecast a 43% critics score. The bar is low: Rotten Tomatoes lists the 1994 film with Jean-Claude Van Damme at 11% and Street Fighter: The Legend of Chun-Li (2009) at 3%.

What critics liked. Kakuchopurei called it a technical knockout, praising its focus on the friendship and rivalry between Ryu and Ken and action that is shot clearly, without distracting fast cuts. The same review enjoyed the 2D animated flashbacks, including Ryu's fight with Sagat, and singled out Jason Momoa's Blanka as a standout. ComicBuzz said Noah Centineo and Andrew Koji make an appealing central pairing and that Cody Rhodes provides welcome comic relief as Guile. MovieWeb reports that critics widely praised Rhodes, Momoa and the hand-drawn flashback animation by Titmouse. Consequence's Liz Shannon Miller said that at its best the film works like a deranged live-action cartoon, which is what she wanted from it, and MovieWeb says IndieWire called it a messy, maximalist reboot destined for cult status.

What they didn't. The Jam Report called it a spectacularly misguided adaptation and said Callina Liang's athletic Chun-Li is undermined by clumsy dialogue and a perfunctory revenge storyline. Variety's Siddhant Adlakha wrote, according to Forbes, that the film starts strongly and then nosedives into nonsense, while The Hollywood Reporter's Frank Scheck said it tries to thread the needle between fan service and welcoming the uninitiated. TL;DR Movie Reviews gave it 2 out of 5 and called it a mess of conflicting tones, although it said the leads are compelling and that the Chun-Li section is some of the best of the film. ComicBuzz felt Momoa's tragic backstory for Blanka was needlessly elaborate and that the film spends so long arranging its tournament that it never delivers a knockout punch. Early social reactions were similarly split, with one critic calling it a silly, fun and fearless good time, according to GamesRadar.

What it is. The PG-13 film is set in 1993, shortly after the release of Street Fighter II. Chun-Li (Liang) recruits the estranged Ryu (Koji) and Ken Masters (Centineo) for a new World Warrior tournament, while M. Bison (David Dastmalchian) has plans of his own. The cast also includes Roman Reigns as Akuma, Cody Rhodes as Guile, Jason Momoa as Blanka, Andrew Schulz, Eric André and 50 Cent, and Forbes lists Olivier Richters as Zangief. It mixes live action with 2D animation and runs 1 hour 59 minutes.

The money. MovieWeb lists domestic opening projections of $30 million to $40 million from Box Office Pro, $22 million to $32 million from Box Office Theory and $27 million to $34 million from Global Box Office. Unofficial reports put the budget between $82 million and $120 million, and Paramount has not confirmed a figure. Street Fighter is a Legendary production released by Paramount.
