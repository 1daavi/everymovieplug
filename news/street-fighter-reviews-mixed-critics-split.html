Street Fighter Reviews Are Mixed as Critics Split on the Fights, the Cast and the Tone

By Maccarthy Albin

October 15, 2026

Updated October 28, 2026

Rotten Tomatoes sits at 38% and Metacritic in the mid-40s, with critics praising the leads and the 2D animation but disagreeing on the writing and tone.

The first reviews of the live-action Street Fighter are in, and they are mixed. The film, directed by Kitao Sakurai (Twisted Metal), had its world premiere in Los Angeles on October 12, and the review embargo lifted on Thursday. It opens in theaters on Friday, October 16. For how it performed once it arrived, see our report on its opening weekend.

Where the Scores Landed

Capcom's adaptation was at 38% on Rotten Tomatoes on Thursday, according to MovieWeb, after debuting at 34%. Forbes says that score was based on 32 reviews, while MovieWeb counts 34, and both note it will move as more reviews post. On Metacritic the film sits in the mid-40s, with World of Reel reporting 45 and MovieWeb 46. Before the embargo lifted, traders on Kalshi had forecast a 43% critics score.

Early scores are fragile. A film with only a few dozen reviews can shift several points as additional outlets file their opinions, and the small differences in review counts between publications show how quickly the numbers change on release day. The consistent message across both aggregators is that the response is neither a disaster nor a triumph, but a divided one.

A Low Bar for Video Game Fighters

The bar is low: Rotten Tomatoes lists the 1994 film with Jean-Claude Van Damme at 11% and Street Fighter: The Legend of Chun-Li (2009) at 3%. Measured against those predecessors, the new film is a clear improvement, even if its score falls short of a critical success.

The history explains why expectations for this version were so closely watched. The franchise has been adapted for the screen before without winning over reviewers, and a more favorable reception would have signaled that a faithful, modern take on the games could succeed where earlier attempts did not. The current response suggests progress rather than a breakthrough.

The Case in the Film’s Favor

Kakuchopurei called it a technical knockout, praising its focus on the friendship and rivalry between Ryu and Ken and action that is shot clearly, without distracting fast cuts. The same review enjoyed the 2D animated flashbacks, including Ryu's fight with Sagat, and singled out Jason Momoa's Blanka as a standout. ComicBuzz said Noah Centineo and Andrew Koji make an appealing central pairing and that Cody Rhodes provides welcome comic relief as Guile.

MovieWeb reports that critics widely praised Rhodes, Momoa and the hand-drawn flashback animation by Titmouse. Consequence's Liz Shannon Miller said that at its best the film works like a deranged live-action cartoon, which is what she wanted from it, and MovieWeb says IndieWire called it a messy, maximalist reboot destined for cult status.

The praise is consistent in its targets. Reviewers who liked the film pointed to clear staging of the fights, the performances of specific cast members and the visual variety offered by the animated sequences. Those strengths are tied to the film’s identity as a game adaptation, and they suggest the movie works best when it leans into its source material.

The Case Against It

The Jam Report called it a spectacularly misguided adaptation and said Callina Liang's athletic Chun-Li is undermined by clumsy dialogue and a perfunctory revenge storyline. Variety's Siddhant Adlakha wrote, according to Forbes, that the film starts strongly and then nosedives into nonsense, while The Hollywood Reporter's Frank Scheck said it tries to thread the needle between fan service and welcoming the uninitiated.

TL;DR Movie Reviews gave it 2 out of 5 and called it a mess of conflicting tones, although it said the leads are compelling and that the Chun-Li section is some of the best of the film. ComicBuzz felt Momoa's tragic backstory for Blanka was needlessly elaborate and that the film spends so long arranging its tournament that it never delivers a knockout punch. Early social reactions were similarly split, with one critic calling it a silly, fun and fearless good time, according to GamesRadar.

The complaints center on writing and balance. Critics who were unconvinced identified the dialogue, the structure and the shifting tone as the main weaknesses, and even some negative reviews found elements to enjoy. That overlap is why the overall response reads as a split rather than a rejection.

Setting, Story and Cast

The PG-13 film is set in 1993, shortly after the release of Street Fighter II. Chun-Li (Liang) recruits the estranged Ryu (Koji) and Ken Masters (Centineo) for a new World Warrior tournament, while M. Bison (David Dastmalchian) has plans of his own.

The cast also includes Roman Reigns as Akuma, Cody Rhodes as Guile, Jason Momoa as Blanka, Andrew Schulz, Eric André and 50 Cent, and Forbes lists Olivier Richters as Zangief. It mixes live action with 2D animation and runs 1 hour 59 minutes.

The large ensemble and the period setting reflect an attempt to honor the games’ roster and their era, which helps explain both the film’s fan appeal and the criticism that it spends considerable time assembling its pieces before the tournament delivers payoff.

What Viewers Can Expect

For audiences deciding whether to buy a ticket, the reviews paint a fairly consistent picture. Viewers who go in looking for clear action, colorful characters and an affectionate nod to the games are likely to find enough to enjoy, while those hoping for tight writing and a steady tone may be disappointed. As more reviews arrive and audience reactions build over the opening weekend, the picture will become clearer.

What the Box Office Might Do

MovieWeb lists domestic opening projections of $30 million to $40 million from Box Office Pro, $22 million to $32 million from Box Office Theory and $27 million to $34 million from Global Box Office. Unofficial reports put the budget between $82 million and $120 million, and Paramount has not confirmed a figure. Street Fighter is a Legendary production released by Paramount.

The range of forecasts reflects real uncertainty. Mixed reviews can dampen a film’s run, but a recognizable brand and a young, game-literate audience often carry an opening weekend regardless of critical response. The wide spread in the budget estimate means the film’s path to profitability remains hard to judge until Paramount releases an official number.
