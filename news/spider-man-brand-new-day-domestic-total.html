Spider-Man: Brand New Day Nears $955 Million Domestically

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The superhero hit is still earning in 1,724 theaters.

Spider-Man: Brand New Day has reached about $955 million at the domestic box office after 68 days in release, according to The Numbers. That leaves it roughly $45 million short of $1 billion in North America, a threshold no film has crossed on the domestic chart.

The film earned about $2.45 million over the October 2 weekend in 1,724 theaters, a 40% decline from the previous weekend. Its worldwide total stands at about $2.5 billion, which places it third on the all-time global chart behind only Avatar and Avengers: Endgame.

Whether it can reach the domestic $1 billion mark will depend on how long theaters keep it on screens. At its current weekly pace, the climb would be slow.

A Launch That Reset Expectations

Sony Pictures and Marvel Studios released the fourth MCU Spider-Man film at the end of July, with Destin Daniel Cretton directing and Tom Holland returning as Peter Parker. The opening weekend brought in roughly $355 million in North America, one of the largest domestic debuts ever recorded and just shy of the mark set by Avengers: Endgame in 2019. Around the world, the first three days produced approximately $927 million, and the weekend lifted overall domestic ticket sales to the biggest three-day total in the history of the market.

Momentum did not fade quickly. The second weekend added about $145 million, one of the strongest sophomore frames on record, and pushed the running domestic total to roughly $655 million after only ten days. That was the fastest any film had reached that level. By day 13 the picture had passed $700 million in North America, a milestone that every previous film in that club needed at least 16 days to reach.

Breaking Records Along the Way

The pace stayed historic through late summer. Brand New Day crossed $900 million domestically on September 5, again faster than any release before it, and it soon overtook the $936 million earned by Star Wars: The Force Awakens. That made it the highest-grossing film ever released in North America. Along the way it also surpassed the $804.7 million lifetime total of Spider-Man: No Way Home, which had been the franchise benchmark for several years.

Internationally, the film passed $1 billion in about twelve days and crossed $2 billion worldwide by mid-August. Produced on a reported budget of $225 million, it has returned many times its cost, and it has become one of the most profitable releases of Holland's career.

How the Weekly Numbers Have Changed

The latest weekend figures show how far the run has moved from its peak. At its widest, the film played in more than 4,500 theaters. This month it is down to 1,724, and its $2.45 million weekend is a small fraction of the $39 million it still earned in late August. Falling theater counts and steady audience attrition are normal for a film in its tenth week, and a 40% weekly decline is in line with the late stage of a long theatrical life.

That decline is the main obstacle to $1 billion. If each coming weekend dropped by a similar percentage, the combined weekend and weekday earnings still ahead would add up to only a few million dollars per week, and the total would taper toward a ceiling well under the $45 million still needed. Reaching the milestone would likely require something unusual, such as a wider return to theaters, a special event screening, or a promotional push tied to another release.

What the Result Means for the Box Office

Even if the film stops short of $1 billion domestically, its run has reshaped the year at the box office. Industry analysts have pointed to it as a major driver of a 2026 domestic market projected to grow roughly 5% in attendance over last year, and it has given theater chains one of the strongest summers in recent memory. For Sony, which shares the character's rights with Marvel Studios, the result reinforces how much audiences still value event releases on the big screen.

The next major test arrives on December 18, when Avengers: Doomsday opens in theaters. Studios will be watching whether the audience energy built by Brand New Day carries over to the next large Marvel release. For now, Spider-Man remains a rare case of a film still earning money in its tenth week and still holding a place in the conversation about the biggest hits of all time.
