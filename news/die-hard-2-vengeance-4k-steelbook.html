Die Hard 2 and Die Hard with a Vengeance Get Their First 4K Releases

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The sequels arrive in 4K SteelBooks on December 1, alongside a new edition of the original.

Die Hard 2 and Die Hard with a Vengeance are coming to 4K Ultra HD for the first time on December 1. 20th Century Studios Home Entertainment announced the releases on October 8, as reported by Media Play News.

What Is in the Box

Each film will come as a SteelBook combo pack with a 4K disc, a regular Blu-ray and a digital code. The original Die Hard, which has been on 4K for years, is also being reissued in a new SteelBook so that all three films match.

A SteelBook is a metal-cased edition of a disc release, usually sold as a collectible with artwork printed on the exterior. The combo format means buyers get several ways to watch from one purchase: the 4K disc for compatible players and televisions, the Blu-ray for standard high-definition setups, and a digital copy for streaming or download. The reissue of the original film in a matching case is aimed at collectors who want the first three entries to sit together on the same shelf.

A Closer Look at the Sequels

Die Hard 2 was directed by Renny Harlin in 1990, and Die Hard with a Vengeance by John McTiernan in 1995. Bruce Willis stars as John McClane in both. The Digital Bits reports that the sequels will be on 100GB discs, with English 5.1 DTS-HD Master Audio and French and Spanish options.

The two films take McClane away from the skyscraper of the 1988 original. Die Hard 2 is set in and around an airport during the Christmas season, while Die Hard with a Vengeance moves the action to the streets of New York City and pairs McClane with a reluctant partner played by Samuel L. Jackson. McTiernan, who directed the first film, returned for the third, which gives the series a bookend structure in terms of its directors.

Why the Disc Capacity Matters

The 100GB figure is significant for home video enthusiasts. Ultra HD Blu-ray discs come in different capacities, and the higher-capacity versions allow a studio to store a high-quality video encoding, lossless audio and other material without heavy compression. The Digital Bits’ report of 100GB discs suggests a format designed to give the films as much room as possible, though the source does not describe the specifics of the video transfer itself. The audio specification, English 5.1 DTS-HD Master Audio, is a lossless format that is commonly used on premium releases.

The Rest of the Franchise

Live Free or Die Hard and A Good Day to Die Hard are not getting new discs, but all five films will receive new artwork at digital retailers, according to BroadwayWorld.

That means the latter two films, released in 2007 and 2013, will remain available in their existing formats, while the digital refresh brings all five titles under a more consistent visual presentation. For buyers hoping to complete a full five-film 4K collection, the announcement is a partial answer, since only three entries are receiving matching SteelBooks.

Questions Still Open

Disney has not yet announced any bonus features, Forbes reports, and fan reaction to the new cover art has been divided.

The lack of announced extras leaves a significant question for collectors. Some buyers choose a physical edition mainly for the supplementary material, including commentaries, featurettes and documentaries, and until Disney confirms what, if anything, will be included, it is unclear whether the new discs carry over older material or arrive without it. The reaction to the cover art is similarly open-ended. Artwork is a matter of taste, and the SteelBook format places particular attention on it, because the cover is the main visual appeal of the product.

A Series Built Around One Cop

The Die Hard series began in 1988 with a story set inside a Los Angeles office tower, and it turned John McClane into one of the best-known action heroes in film. Each sequel placed him in a different location and gave him a new set of adversaries, which gave the franchise a recognizable template: an ordinary police officer in the wrong place at the wrong time. Bringing the second and third films to 4K completes the first trilogy’s presence in the format and gives viewers an opportunity to watch the early run of the series at the highest home video quality currently on the market.

Timing and What to Expect

With a December 1 release date, the discs arrive at the start of the holiday season, a period when physical media releases are often positioned as gifts. That timing is a natural fit for a franchise whose first film is widely associated with Christmas, and the second film is also set during the holiday period. The studio announced the titles on October 8, giving buyers several weeks to decide before the discs reach stores.

Pricing and retailer exclusives are not covered in the reports used here, so those details remain to be confirmed. Interested buyers should look to the studio’s announcements and to individual retailers for final information on price, availability and pre-order options as the release date approaches.
