Denzel Washington to Star in Christopher McQuarrie Crime Thriller for October 2027

By Maccarthy Albin

October 21, 2026

He plays a dying former detective released from prison to make amends with his family.

Denzel Washington is teaming with Christopher McQuarrie on a new crime thriller. Amazon MGM Studios and United Artists will release the film in theaters on October 1, 2027, according to The Playlist and JoBlo.

The pairing brings together an Oscar-winning actor and a director best known for large-scale action, and the early release date signals that the studio sees the project as a priority. With production slated to begin soon, the film is already moving from announcement toward the cameras.

A Dying Detective Seeks Amends

Washington plays a former police detective serving a life sentence who learns he has terminal brain cancer. He is granted compassionate release with six months to live and returns to the estranged daughter he left behind and a grandson he never knew he had. He wants to make amends, but his past has not forgotten him, per JoBlo.

The premise combines two familiar crime-story elements: a character racing against a deadline and a man trying to repair relationships damaged long ago. The ticking clock of six months raises the stakes for every decision, while the arrival of a grandson he has never met gives the story an emotional anchor beyond the criminal plot. The line that his past has not forgotten him points to a threat from his earlier life, which suggests that the film will balance family drama with pursuit and danger.

Compassionate release, the mechanism that frees the character, is a real legal provision that allows some terminally ill prisoners to leave custody near the end of their lives. Using it as the starting point gives the story a grounded entry into the plot, and it frames the central question of what a person can repair, and what he can only acknowledge, when time is nearly gone.

McQuarrie Steps Away From Mission: Impossible

McQuarrie, who directed the recent Mission: Impossible films including The Final Reckoning, directs from a script by Scott Barkan and Gregg Zehentner. JoBlo says production is slated to begin in New Jersey in November, and the role of Washington's daughter is still being cast. The film is untitled, and JoBlo notes it was formerly called The Frame.

McQuarrie won an Oscar for writing The Usual Suspects and directed Jack Reacher before taking over the Mission: Impossible series, so a grounded crime story is not a complete departure from his background. The project does differ in scale from the globe-spanning spectacle of his recent work, and it gives him the chance to work in a more character-driven register. McQuarrie directed four Mission: Impossible films in a row, starting with Rogue Nation in 2015 and continuing through Fallout and Dead Reckoning to The Final Reckoning, so this project marks his first announced film outside that series in a long while. The script, credited to Barkan and Zehentner rather than to McQuarrie himself, adds a further new element to the collaboration.

Casting and Production Details

The casting of Washington's on-screen daughter remains open, which makes it one of the more closely watched details as production approaches. The role is central to the story, since the reunion between father and daughter drives the emotional arc, and the actor chosen will share many of the film's key scenes with Washington. New Jersey is the reported location for the start of production, and no further location details have been given. The film remains untitled, and its working name has already changed once, so the final title could still differ from either version.

A Career Built on Crime Stories

Washington won the Oscar for best actor for Training Day, in which he played a corrupt detective, and he has returned to law enforcement and underworld stories repeatedly across his career. He has also worked in the Equalizer series, a franchise that gave him an ongoing action role in his later years. A film about a former detective who has served time connects naturally with that history, and it places him opposite a director with a strong record in suspense.

The Rest of Washington's Schedule

Washington has other films ahead. The Cinemaholic lists Here Comes the Flood, a Netflix heist thriller with Robert Pattinson and Daisy Edgar-Jones, for 2027, and Screen Rant reports it has been delayed to that year. The same list has Black Panther 3 from Ryan Coogler on December 15, 2028, and The Equalizer 4 in development with no date. It also says a Netflix Hannibal film directed by Antoine Fuqua is on hold after Netflix halted pre-production in June to sort out the budget.

The list shows how full his calendar has become. Several projects are in different stages, from a film awaiting release to one in development and another paused over cost. The new McQuarrie thriller sits among them as a project with a firm date and a start of production only weeks away, which gives it a clearer path than some of the others. The October 1, 2027 date also places the film in the autumn corridor traditionally associated with adult-oriented dramas and thrillers.
