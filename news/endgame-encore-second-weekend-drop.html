Avengers: Endgame Encore Falls 83% in Second Weekend

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The extended re-release dropped from first place to seventh.

Avengers: Endgame Encore fell 83% in its second weekend, earning about $4.4 million in 3,000 theaters and dropping to seventh place. The week before, the extended re-release had been No. 1 on the domestic chart.

The fall is steep, but typical for event re-releases that rely on a first-weekend rush of fans. Daily grosses over the weekend ranged from about $2.0 million on Saturday to $1.3 million on Sunday.

The re-release has still helped the film's lifetime numbers. Avengers: Endgame stood at about $895.5 million domestically as of Tuesday, October 6, according to The Numbers. Earlier this month, the Encore cut helped push the film back ahead of Avatar as the highest-grossing movie of all time worldwide. Read our earlier report on the record.

What the Encore Edition Offered

Disney and Marvel Studios brought the 2019 finale back to theaters on September 25 with a new title and several additions. The Encore cut includes a custom introduction, new footage that connects to the upcoming Avengers: Doomsday, and a special end tag that was shown only in IMAX and Infinity Vision auditoriums. The runtime grew to about 185 minutes, roughly four minutes longer than the original. Co-director Joe Russo described the new material as part of a plan to link the earlier film to the next chapter of the saga, which opens on December 18.

Advance sales signaled strong demand. Industry tracking reported the biggest presales of the century for a theatrical re-release, ahead of the earlier mark held by Titanic 3D. That early interest set up a high first weekend and, as a result, a very large percentage drop in the weekend that followed.

A Strong First Weekend Sets the Comparison

The opening weekend earned about $26 million domestically and roughly $60 million overseas, for a global total near $86 million. That made it the second-largest worldwide weekend ever for a re-release, behind only the 2012 reissue of Titanic 3D. Premium screens drove much of the business, with IMAX and Infinity Vision locations responsible for about 60% of the domestic take. Analyst David A. Gross noted that re-releases rarely earn more than $10 million, which shows how far above the norm the first weekend was.

Seen against that opening, the $4.4 million second weekend is less alarming than the percentage suggests. A drop of 83% on a re-release that opened at $26 million still leaves a number that many standard re-issues never reach in their first weekend. The decline also reflects the nature of the event. Fans who wanted the new footage and the premium-format experience bought tickets right away, while the wider audience that sees most new releases had already seen the film or had little reason to return.

How the Record Changed Hands

The biggest effect of the re-release came on the global chart. After the first Encore weekend, the film's worldwide total stood at about $2.885 billion, just behind Avatar. Continued overseas business then carried it past its rival, with outlets reporting a total near $2.925 billion against roughly $2.923 billion for Avatar, a margin of only a couple of million dollars. The lead is narrow, and the two films have traded the top position before: Endgame first passed Avatar in 2019, and later reissues of James Cameron's film took the title back.

Because the margin is so thin, the all-time ranking could change again if either film returns to theaters.

Where the Domestic Chart Stands

The theater count of about 3,000 stayed roughly steady into the second weekend, but the audience did not, and the film slipped behind new openers and holdovers. Verity took first place, followed by Resident Evil and Heart of the Beast. For the re-release, the domestic total of about $895.5 million as of October 6 is a useful reminder of how much the Encore run added to a film that had already earned a large sum in 2019.

With the record secured and its main job done, the Encore edition is expected to wind down over the coming weeks. Its larger purpose is to keep fans engaged with the franchise as December approaches, and the early numbers suggest that goal was met.
