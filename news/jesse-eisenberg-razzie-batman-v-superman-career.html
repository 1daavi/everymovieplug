Jesse Eisenberg Says His Razzie Win for Batman v Superman Cost Him Studio Roles

By Maccarthy Albin

October 18, 2026

The actor says the industry did not want him in a superhero film after playing Lex Luthor.

Jesse Eisenberg says his Razzie win for Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice damaged his career. “I definitely couldn't get studio parts anymore after my Razzie win. The industry did not want me in a superhero film,” he said, according to Entertainment Report. Variety headlined its own report that winning the Razzie damaged his career.

Eisenberg won the Golden Raspberry Award for playing Lex Luthor in the 2016 film. Entertainment Report does not name the outlet or date of the interview.

A Familiar Complaint

It is not the first time he has said this. On the Armchair Expert podcast in December 2024, he said the film was “so poorly received” and that he was too, adding: “I genuinely think it actually hurt my career in a real way,” according to Gamereactor. He said he does not read reviews or press and so was unaware of how poorly it was received.

That detail matters because it suggests Eisenberg learned about the reaction secondhand, through the way the industry treated him after the film opened, rather than through the critical coverage itself. His comments across two separate appearances, nearly two years apart, point to a consistent view of how the experience shaped the years that followed.

Why a Razzie Draws So Much Attention

The Golden Raspberry Awards were created as a tongue-in-cheek counterpart to the Academy Awards, naming the worst achievements in film each year. They are announced the day before the Oscars and are decided by a voting membership rather than a panel of industry professionals. Winners are rarely present to collect the trophy, and the ceremony has always been treated as a joke by most of Hollywood.

Even so, the headlines that come with a Razzie can linger. For an actor, the label “Razzie winner” can travel alongside a name in news coverage, in interviews and in online discussion long after the film has left theaters. Eisenberg’s remarks describe exactly that kind of lingering effect, whatever the intent of the award itself.

The Role That Divided Audiences

Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice was directed by Zack Snyder and released in March 2016. It starred Ben Affleck as Batman, Henry Cavill as Superman and Gal Gadot as Wonder Woman, and it served as a major step in building the shared DC film universe. Eisenberg was cast as Lex Luthor, the wealthy and manipulative rival who has been one of Superman’s best-known adversaries in comics for decades.

His version of the character was a notable departure from earlier screen portrayals. Rather than a calm, calculating businessman, Eisenberg played Luthor as a young, restless and unpredictable tech heir, speaking in rapid, jittery bursts. Some viewers saw this as a bold reinvention, while others felt it clashed with the tone of the rest of the film. That split in reaction ultimately framed how his performance was discussed and, eventually, how it was recognized by the Razzies.

The film earned a very large worldwide box office total, yet its critical reception was sharply divided, and conversation around it has remained active ever since. Eisenberg later appeared as Luthor again in a brief moment in the 2017 team-up film Justice League, which kept the character connected to the larger DC story even as the franchise changed direction.

A Career Built Beyond the Blockbuster

Eisenberg’s reputation was established well before Batman v Superman. He earned an Oscar nomination for his portrayal of Mark Zuckerberg in The Social Network in 2010, a performance that made him one of the most recognizable actors of his generation. He has also worked steadily on stage as both a playwright and a performer, and he has appeared in a range of comedies, thrillers and dramas.

In recent years he has put increasing energy into work behind the camera. He wrote and directed When You Finish Saving the World in 2022 and followed it with A Real Pain in 2024, a film in which he also starred opposite Kieran Culkin. The latter drew strong awards attention, with Culkin winning the Oscar for best supporting actor and Eisenberg receiving a nomination for his original screenplay. Those results show that the studio-role drought he describes has not stopped him from finding acclaim elsewhere.

What Comes Next

Eisenberg’s directorial effort The Debut recently premiered in London, with Julianne Moore attending, according to Variety. THR says his next project is a TV series.

The move toward independent filmmaking and television fits the pattern he has described, in which smaller, character-driven projects have become the main outlet for his work. Whether or not the major studios return to him, his own comments make clear that he sees the Batman v Superman chapter as a turning point, and his current projects reflect a career that has continued in a different direction.
