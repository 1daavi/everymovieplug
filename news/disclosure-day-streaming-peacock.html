Disclosure Day Arrives on Peacock October 9

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The theatrical hit makes its streaming debut after a $111 million domestic run.

Disclosure Day began streaming on Peacock on October 9. The Universal Pictures film stars Emily Blunt, Josh O'Connor, Colman Domingo and Tommy Martinez.

The film was a solid theatrical performer. It had grossed about $111.3 million domestically by the weekend of July 10, its fifth weekend in release, according to The Numbers.

October is a busy month for fall premieres and horror titles across the major services. Boston.com has a full list of what is arriving on each one this month.

What the film is about

Steven Spielberg directed and produced Disclosure Day from a screenplay by David Koepp, based on a story by Spielberg. Emily Blunt plays Margaret Fairchild, a TV meteorologist, and Josh O'Connor plays Dr. Daniel Kellner, a cybersecurity specialist and whistleblower. Together they set out to reveal the existence of extraterrestrial life to the world, while a government contractor tries to stop them from getting the secret out.

The cast also includes Colin Firth as Noah Scanlon, head of the Wardex Corporation, Eve Hewson as Jane Blankenship, and Colman Domingo as Hugo Wakefield, a former Wardex official who becomes an advocate for disclosure. Janusz Kamiński shot the film, and John Williams composed the score, marking his thirtieth collaboration with Spielberg.

Theatrical run and reception

The film premiered in Paris on June 2, 2026, and opened in the United States on June 12 through Universal Pictures, with Amblin Entertainment producing. It runs 145 minutes. Reported figures put the production budget at $115 million and the final box office at $116.6 million domestically and $246.8 million worldwide, roughly twice its budget. Forbes described its earlier worldwide total of about $232 million as underwhelming.

Critics were generally favorable. The film holds 79% on Rotten Tomatoes from 442 reviews and 74 out of 100 on Metacritic, with audiences giving it a B on CinemaScore. Reviewers praised Spielberg's direction, Blunt's performance, Williams's score and the visuals, while some found the plot muddled or the effects uneven.

Where to watch

Before arriving on Peacock, the film was available to rent or buy digitally on July 21, and on 4K Ultra HD, Blu-ray and DVD from Universal Pictures Home Entertainment on August 25. The Peacock debut on October 9 follows those releases and gives subscribers a way to watch without paying extra.
