Avatar: Seven Havens Premieres and Is Renewed for Books 3 and 4

By Maccarthy Albin

October 11, 2026

Paramount+ also announced a new animated series, Avatar Kuruk: Wave of Chaos.

Paramount+ did not wait long to commit to its new Avatar. Avatar: Seven Havens has been renewed for Books 3 and 4 at Paramount+. The announcement was made at the show’s New York Comic Con panel on Friday, October 9, the same day its first three episodes arrived on the streamer, according to Variety.

A Renewal Before the First Finale

Three more episodes arrive each Friday through October 30. Book 2 does not yet have a premiere date. TVLine calls the renewal quick, since the series had only just premiered. Paramount+ says Books 3 and 4 will continue the story of Avatar Pavi and her twin sister Nisha.

The speed of the decision is unusual, though it may be less surprising than it appears. Reports about the original order describe a 26-episode commitment split into two books of 13 episodes each, which means the platform had already planned a longer run before the first episode aired. The new renewals extend that plan to a total of four books, giving the creators a clearer runway to tell a story that has been mapped out in advance.

The Creators and the Setting

The series was created by Avatar: The Last Airbender co-creators Michael Dante DiMartino and Bryan Konietzko, who were joined at the panel by executive producer Ethan Spaulding, co-executive producer Sehaj Sethi and voice actors Saheli Khan, Aishu Devan and Major Curda. It is set 225 years after the end of The Legend of Korra, in a world shattered by a cataclysm. Pavi, voiced by Khan, is a young Earthbender who discovers she is the new Avatar, only to learn that the title marks her as humanity’s destroyer, not its savior.

In the story, Pavi is hunted by both human and spirit enemies and reunites with her long-lost twin, Nisha, voiced by Devan. Together they try to uncover the mystery of their origins and protect the Seven Havens, the last strongholds of civilization, before those places collapse. The premise reverses the franchise’s usual hope, since the world views its Avatar as a threat rather than a hero.

Who Is Behind the Voices

Khan, who makes her first major screen appearance in the series, leads a voice cast that also includes Akshay Khanna as Captain Karthik, Major Curda as Jae, Sakina Jaffrey as Agam, Darren Barnet as Daemin and Dianne Doan as Zi. Dee Bradley Baker, a veteran of the franchise, voices the animal companions Geet and Ruhi. At the panel, Devan said she first heard about the project under a codename that described a superhero-style series, and only later learned it was an Avatar show.

A Different Look for the Franchise

The visual style marks a clear change from earlier entries. Previewers have described a refined, cel-shaded look with rounded, almost chibi-like character designs, and early reviews have been mixed about the animation while praising the story. One review of the first three episodes called the plot compelling despite what it described as mediocre animation, and it noted that Pavi and Nisha approach being the Avatar in sharply different ways, with Nisha focused on bending and Pavi on the compassionate, spiritual side of the role.

The First Book at a Glance

The first book runs for 13 half-hour episodes. Paramount+ is releasing them in weekly batches, with the first three on October 9 and the finale, titled “The Root Chakra,” arriving on October 30. The structure lets viewers binge each batch while still giving the show a few weeks of conversation before the book closes. A second book has been confirmed as part of the broader order, though its premiere date has not been announced.

Early reviews describe a story built on contrast. Pavi and Nisha are twins who have been separated for years, and their different temperaments drive much of the early drama, while Captain Karthik is presented as a protective leader whose motives may be more complicated than they first seem. Jae, voiced by Curda, supplies much of the humor, and his co-stars joked in press interviews that fans are already calling him the new Sokka. That kind of ensemble is central to what made the original series popular, and it suggests the creators are deliberately building on the franchise’s strengths.

More Avatar on the Way

Paramount+ also announced a new animated series, Avatar Kuruk: Wave of Chaos, about the teenage Avatar Kuruk. A Seven Havens podcast and a Seven Havens expansion for the game Avatar Legends: The Fighting Game are also coming, The Hollywood Reporter reports. Paramount+ is now part of the company created by the Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery merger, which you can read about in our report.

The announcements show a studio treating the property as a long-term franchise instead of a single series. The original Avatar: The Last Airbender ran on Nickelodeon from 2005 to 2008, and The Legend of Korra followed from 2012 to 2014. A live-action adaptation reached Netflix in 2024, and now Seven Havens and the Kuruk series will expand the animated side of the franchise in two different directions, one forward in time and one into its distant past.

Why It Matters to Paramount+

The renewal gives the streamer a rare piece of long-term programming. A franchise with a loyal following, an established fan base across several generations and a steady stream of spinoffs offers predictable demand, and it makes sense for the platform to secure the next several seasons early. For fans, the practical effect is simple: the story of Pavi and Nisha will be allowed to unfold over four books rather than being cut short, and a second Avatar series gives viewers another reason to stay subscribed.
