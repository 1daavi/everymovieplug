Verity Debuts at No. 1 With $32.6 Million

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

The new release took the top spot in 3,510 theaters over the October 2 weekend.

Verity opened at No. 1 at the domestic box office, earning about $32.6 million over the weekend of October 2 to 4 in 3,510 theaters. That works out to an average of roughly $9,293 per theater.

The debut was strongest on opening day. Friday brought in about $15.1 million, including Thursday previews, before daily grosses eased to roughly $10 million on Saturday and $7.5 million on Sunday.

Weekend Top Three

  1. Verity: $32.6 million (opening weekend)
  2. Resident Evil: $12.5 million
  3. Heart of the Beast: $11.0 million

By Tuesday, October 6, Verity had grossed about $38.0 million after five days in release, and it remained the No. 1 film on the daily chart.

An Adaptation Built on a Bestseller

Amazon MGM Studios released Verity, an R-rated psychological thriller directed by Michael Showalter. Anne Hathaway, Dakota Johnson and Josh Hartnett star in the film, which is based on the novel by Colleen Hoover. The story follows a struggling writer who is hired to help a famous author finish her book, and who soon finds that the project pulls her into a much darker situation. The production carried a reported budget of about $40 million, a modest figure for a studio release with this much star power.

How the Opening Compares

Among films adapted from Hoover's books, Verity now holds the second-largest domestic opening. It trails only It Ends With Us, which launched with about $50 million in August 2024. It comfortably clears Reminders of Him, which opened to roughly $18 million, and Regretting You, which started with $13.7 million in October 2025. The film also added around $30 million overseas, lifting its global debut to about $62.6 million.

The result is also a milestone for its leads. It marks Hathaway's third No. 1 opening of 2026, following The Devil Wears Prada 2 and The Odyssey. For Johnson, it is her strongest start in some time. Her biggest domestic opening since the Fifty Shades films had been Materialists, which earned $36.5 million in total domestic receipts, so Verity reached a comparable number in only three days.

Strong Turnout Despite Mixed Reception

Reviews were not kind to the film. It holds a 33% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes, and audiences gave it a C+ on CinemaScore, a grade that often signals limited word of mouth. Even so, the turnout was solid, which suggests that the appeal of the source novel and the cast carried much of the opening. Films in this category tend to draw their biggest crowds early, so the steep drop from Friday to Sunday fits the pattern. The key question now is how large the second-weekend decline will be, since a fast fall would limit the final total.

The Rest of the Chart

The holdovers behaved as expected. Resident Evil stayed in second place with $12.5 million, a drop of about 46% in its third weekend, bringing its domestic total to roughly $125 million. Heart of the Beast took third with $11.0 million, down about 45% in its second weekend, which is a healthy hold for a new release.

The weekend's other major opener struggled. Tom Cruise's Digger, directed by Alejandro G. Iñárritu, debuted with about $8 million and landed in fifth place. Next to it, Verity started with more than four times the domestic total and about three times the worldwide figure, which made the contrast one of the main stories of the weekend.

Why the Number Matters

Mid-budget thrillers aimed at adult audiences have had an uneven record in theaters, and studios often treat a strong opening as proof that the genre can still draw crowds without a franchise attached. A $32.6 million start on an original-style property gives distributors a useful data point as they plan the remaining autumn calendar, when similar titles will compete for the same viewers.

What Comes Next

With a production cost near $40 million and more than $62 million already earned around the world, Verity has a strong start on its path to profitability. Its final result will depend on how quickly audiences fade in the coming weeks and on how the film performs in markets that are still rolling out. Its $38.0 million domestic total after five days shows that midweek business has been steady, and the next weekend will show whether the thriller can build on that.
