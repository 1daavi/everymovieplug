Dune: Part Three Early Screenings Draw “Very Positive” Reactions as December 18 Release Nears

By Maccarthy Albin

October 18, 2026

Jason Momoa called Timothée Chalamet's performance “maybe some of the best stuff I've seen anyone do – ever.”

Early word on Dune: Part Three is strong. Variety's Clayton Davis reports the reaction from the film's first small-group screenings has been “very positive,” according to World of Reel. The film opens in theaters on December 18.

The reports come roughly two months before the film reaches audiences and give the first indication of how the third film in Denis Villeneuve's Dune series is landing with the people who have seen it. As with any early screening, the responses are anecdotal and come from limited audiences, but they set a hopeful tone for a release that carries high expectations.

Reading the Early Reactions

World of Reel says the screenings were held in late September, and that a source connected to Warner Bros. who saw an internal screening in August said the room was “blown away.” Jason Momoa, who plays Duncan Idaho, praised Timothée Chalamet: “Maybe some of the best stuff I've seen anyone do – ever.” The August screening was a rough cut running over three hours, per the same report. Movie Insider lists a running time of 165 minutes.

The difference between the two figures is worth understanding. A rough cut is an early assembly of a film that has not yet been trimmed, scored or finished, and it typically runs longer than the final version. The listed running time of 165 minutes, or two hours and 45 minutes, suggests that the film has been tightened since that August viewing, though the final length will not be certain until the studio confirms it.

The praise for Chalamet also carries a particular significance. Paul Atreides is the center of the story, and the film depends heavily on the lead performance. A castmate describing the performance in such strong terms offers a hint of how central it may prove, though it remains one person's opinion rather than a critical verdict.

From Part Two to Messiah

Denis Villeneuve directs from a script he co-wrote with Brian K. Vaughan, adapting Frank Herbert's Dune Messiah. The story is set 12 years after Part Two, with Paul Atreides as emperor facing threats inside and outside his palace, according to Backstage.

Herbert published Dune Messiah in 1969 as the second novel in his series, and it is a shorter, more reflective book than its predecessor. Where the first novel follows Paul's rise, the sequel examines the consequences of that rise and the weight of ruling an empire whose conquest was bloody. Dune: Part Two, released in 2024, ended with Paul claiming the throne, which sets up the political situation that this film begins from. The twelve-year gap gives the story room to show how power has changed the characters.

Villeneuve's Return to Arrakis

Villeneuve's earlier Dune film arrived in 2021, followed by Part Two in 2024, and he is also known for Arrival and Blade Runner 2049, both large-scale science fiction films with a measured, atmospheric style. His return for a third installment gives the series a consistent directorial voice from start to finish, and the early reaction suggests that approach has carried through to the new film.

Familiar Faces and a New Arrival

Chalamet, Zendaya, Rebecca Ferguson, Florence Pugh, Anya Taylor-Joy, Javier Bardem, Josh Brolin and Jason Momoa return, and Robert Pattinson joins as Scytale.

The returning ensemble includes performers who built the earlier films' emotional core, with Zendaya as Chani and Pugh as Princess Irulan among them. Pattinson's addition is notable because Scytale is a figure from the novel who plays a significant role in the intrigue surrounding Paul's court. Bringing in an actor of his profile signals that the filmmakers consider the character an important part of the story's tension.

Premium Formats and Early Fan Access

Tickets for IMAX 70mm and other premium formats went on sale August 18, alongside a new trailer, according to Gizmodo. “Dune Insiders” screenings are set for December 14 in 70mm IMAX, December 15 in other IMAX theaters and December 17 in other premium formats.

The staggered schedule rewards audiences who prefer the largest formats, giving them access to the film several days before its general opening. The early on-sale date for premium tickets also reflects the demand that the franchise has generated, since these screenings are limited in number and often sell out well in advance. Viewers who want to see the film in 70mm IMAX should check availability early.

A Crowded Opening Day

The film opens the same day as Avengers: Doomsday. Two major releases arriving together will compete for premium screens, particularly large-format theaters, and the overlap makes the early positive reaction to Dune: Part Three a notable piece of momentum for the studio as the date approaches.

For now, the picture is limited to what small audiences and a few connected sources have said. Full critical reviews have not yet been published, the final running time has not been confirmed by the studio, and the box office performance of the film against a major rival remains to be seen. Viewers deciding how to see the film will have more information as December 18 draws closer and as the first public reactions begin to arrive.
