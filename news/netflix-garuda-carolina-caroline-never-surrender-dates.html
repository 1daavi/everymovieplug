Netflix Sets Dates for Garuda: Dare to Dream, Carolina Caroline and Never Surrender

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

Three films get Netflix dates, starting next week with an animated football story.

Netflix has locked in release dates for three films. The first arrives in less than a week: the Indonesian animated feature Garuda: Dare to Dream streams on October 15 in most major regions, including the US and UK, according to What's on Netflix.

The three titles could hardly be more different in tone or origin. One is a family-friendly animated sports story from Indonesia, one is an R-rated American crime film that has already played in theaters, and one is an Indonesian action thriller made for the service. Together they show the range of a streaming schedule that mixes international productions, acquired releases and Netflix Originals, and they give subscribers three distinct options across the next several weeks.

A Football Dream With a Mystical Twist

The film follows Putra, a 13-year-old with asthma who dreams of playing for Indonesia's national team. After he is mocked at trials, a glowing Garuda spirit named Gaga gives him a mystical jersey that boosts his speed, power and skill. Ronny Gani, who previously worked as an animator on Marvel films, directs, with Keanu Azka and Kristo Immanuel voicing Putra and Gaga. It competed in the animation section at the 2026 Shanghai International Film Festival and won two awards at the 56th Giffoni Film Festival.

The title draws on a figure with deep roots in Indonesian culture. The Garuda, a mythical bird, appears on the country's national emblem, and the national football team is widely known by the nickname of the same name. Building a children's story around that symbol gives the film a strong local identity while keeping its themes accessible to viewers elsewhere. A young player facing doubt and physical limitations, then finding confidence through an unlikely source of help, is a storyline that works across languages and borders.

The festival record adds to the film's credibility. Competing in the animation section in Shanghai and winning two awards at Giffoni, an event focused on films for young audiences, suggests that the film has been well received by juries and viewers who specialize in this kind of work. Gani's experience as an animator on Marvel productions also connects the film to the international animation industry, and it points to the skills that Indonesian artists are applying to homegrown stories.

The streaming release on October 15 covers most major regions, including the US and UK. That wide availability gives the film the chance to reach audiences well beyond Southeast Asia, which is often the main benefit of a Netflix debut for a production of this kind.

A Crime Spree Across the American Southeast

Samara Weaving and Kyle Gallner star in the R-rated Carolina Caroline, directed by Adam Carter Rehmeier. Weaving plays Caroline, a young woman from a small Texas town who teams up with a con man named Oliver for a crime spree across the American Southeast while searching for the mother who left her. Magnolia Pictures released it in US theaters on June 5, and it reaches Netflix in the US on November 6. It runs 1 hour 47 minutes and holds a 90% critics score on Rotten Tomatoes.

The film is a road story at heart, pairing a search for family with the energy of a crime caper. Weaving is known for horror and dark comedy, including Ready or Not, and Gallner has appeared in several well-known horror films, so the pairing brings experience with genre material that mixes tension and humor. The premise of a young woman from a small town drawn into a series of crimes by a charming stranger recalls a long tradition of outlaw couples on screen, though the emotional core here is the search for a missing parent.

The release path is typical of independent films. Magnolia Pictures gave it a theatrical run on June 5, and its arrival on Netflix in the US on November 6 follows a few months later, a window that lets a smaller film reach a much larger audience after its time in cinemas. The 90% critics score on Rotten Tomatoes suggests that reviewers have responded well, which may help it find viewers on the service.

A Quiet Village Turns Violent

The third title, Never Surrender, is an Indonesian Netflix Original that follows a grandfather in a small Central Java village who suddenly has to raise his two grandchildren, until armed criminals from Jakarta attack their home. Sidharta Tata directs, with Lukman Sardi leading the cast. It arrives on November 12.

The setup places an ordinary family in a situation that tests their resilience. A caregiver already adjusting to an unexpected responsibility is confronted by outside violence, and the contrast between a peaceful rural village and criminals arriving from the capital gives the story a clear sense of conflict. Casting an older man as the central figure offers a different kind of hero from the younger leads who usually headline action films, and it places the emphasis on protecting family rather than on spectacle alone.

Why These Three Releases Matter

Two of the three films come from Indonesia, which reflects the increasing role of Indonesian storytelling on global streaming platforms. Netflix's willingness to schedule an animated sports film and an action thriller from the country within a month of each other suggests that it sees an audience for local-language content that travels well. Carolina Caroline reflects the service's parallel strategy of acquiring films that have already completed a theatrical run.

For viewers planning their watchlists, the schedule is simple. The animated film arrives first on October 15, the crime film follows on November 6, and the Indonesian thriller closes the run on November 12. Each targets a different audience, from families to adults looking for a road-trip crime story and viewers who prefer action, so there is little overlap among them.

Release dates for streaming titles can vary by region, and the details reported so far apply to the regions named in the reports, chiefly the US and, for Garuda: Dare to Dream, the UK. Viewers elsewhere should check their local Netflix listings as each date approaches, since availability and rights arrangements can differ from one territory to another, particularly for a film such as Carolina Caroline that was released theatrically by a separate distributor.
