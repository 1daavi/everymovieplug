Resident Evil Tops $127 Million as Holdovers Stay Strong

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

Several recent releases are holding steady behind newcomer Verity.

Behind the new releases, several films from recent weeks continue to build their totals at the domestic box office. The October 2 weekend belonged to Verity, but the titles beneath it tell a quieter and more useful story about how audiences are spreading their attention across a crowded autumn calendar.

Where They Stand

  • Resident Evil: $12.5 million over the October 2 weekend, down 46%. It reached about $127.9 million after 19 days in release.
  • Heart of the Beast: $11.0 million for the weekend, down 45%, with about $40.8 million total after 12 days.
  • Primetime: $8.0 million for the weekend, down 58%, bringing its total to about $34.1 million after 10 days.
  • Forgotten Island: $7.2 million for the weekend, down 45%, with about $24.9 million total after 12 days.

Resident Evil remains the clear leader of the group, and it is the only one of the four past $100 million domestically.

Resident Evil Keeps Climbing

Sony's reboot, directed by Zach Cregger and produced with Constantin, opened in mid-September well ahead of expectations, with a debut of roughly $60 million. Its second weekend added $23.3 million, enough to push it past $100 million in North America and make it the first film in the long-running video game franchise to reach that mark. A drop of about 46% in its third weekend is a moderate decline for a horror-leaning title, since films in the genre often fall harder once the first wave of fans has seen them. The picture is also within reach of the domestic total of Cregger's earlier hit Weapons, which ended its run at about $151.5 million, though it will need steady weekday business to get there.

Heart of the Beast Settles Into a Steady Run

Paramount's Heart of the Beast, starring Brad Pitt, opened to about $20 million in late September. Its $11.0 million second weekend was a 45% decline, a respectable hold for a film that started with a modest opening. The challenge is the cost. The film carried a reported production budget of $82 million, so it will need a long theatrical life and strong international business to move into profit. A total near $41 million after twelve days is a reasonable base, but the studio will be looking for small weekly drops over the next few weeks.

Primetime Faces the Steepest Drop

A24's Primetime, with Robert Pattinson playing television host Chris Hansen, started with about $19.2 million from fewer than 2,900 theaters, a strong per-theater figure for the distributor. The film was made on a modest budget and drew good reviews, which helps its profitability picture even though its second weekend fell 58%, the sharpest decline of the four. Adult-skewing dramas often lose a large share of their opening-weekend crowd quickly and then stabilize, so the more meaningful number may be how it performs on weekdays and in its third frame.

Forgotten Island Holds Better Than It Opened

The DreamWorks Animation release from Universal, directed by Joel Crawford and Januel Mercado, debuted below expectations with about $12.8 million, even though it earned the best audience grade of that weekend, an A CinemaScore, along with a 94% fresh score from critics. Its 45% second-weekend decline is in line with its competitors, and family films frequently keep earning through school holidays and weekend matinees. A total of about $24.9 million after twelve days is modest, but the strong reception gives it a chance to keep adding steadily.

What to Watch Next

Three measures will matter most over the coming weeks: the size of each film's third-weekend drop, the number of theaters that keep it on screens, and how much international revenue each one adds. A film that holds above 1,500 locations and loses less than half its audience each week can still gain tens of millions of dollars before it leaves theaters. Those that lose screens quickly tend to stall far sooner.

A Healthy Market Overall

The wider market gives these numbers context. At the end of September, the domestic box office stood at about $7.8 billion for the year, roughly 19% ahead of the same point in 2025. In that kind of environment, several films can play for weeks without cannibalizing one another, and theaters can keep multiple titles on their schedules. The next few weekends will show whether these holdovers can keep their declines near 40% to 45% as new releases arrive, or whether the pace of attrition speeds up.
