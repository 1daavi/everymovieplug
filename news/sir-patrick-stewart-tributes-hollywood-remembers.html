Hollywood Remembers Sir Patrick Stewart: Tributes From Marvel, Star Trek and Co-Stars

The actor behind Captain Picard and Professor X has died at 86, and the film, television and theatre worlds are paying their respects.

Sir Patrick Stewart, the English actor who gave the world Captain Jean-Luc Picard and Professor Charles Xavier, has died at the age of 86. Within hours of the news, studios, former co-stars and the institutions that shaped his career began posting tributes online.

His spokesperson, Kelly Bush Novak, said Stewart died on Wednesday, October 28, at his home in Los Angeles. The announcement came a day later through his official Instagram account. It described “a brief and poignant journey” with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia, according to ABC News and Yahoo Entertainment.

Marvel and the X-Men Family

Marvel Studios was among the first to respond, posting on X that its team was deeply saddened by his passing. The studio said Stewart brought Professor X to life with grace, intelligence and humanity across X-Men, Logan and the upcoming Avengers: Doomsday, and described him as a generous collaborator and a true gentleman. It also sent condolences to his wife, the singer Sunny Ozell, and his children.

Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige recalled the first time he saw Stewart on the set of the original X-Men. After years of knowing him as Picard, Feige said, he found the real man warm, generous and quietly authoritative, with a sharp sense of humor. Feige also pointed to the many people Stewart mentored, per ABC News. Avengers: Doomsday directors Joe and Anthony Russo wrote on Instagram that Stewart was one of their childhood heroes and thanked him for teaching them about humanity.

Hugh Jackman, who shared the screen with Stewart for nearly two decades as Wolverine, posted photos of the two together. He called Stewart one of the greats of stage and screen and a tremendous friend, adding that he had learned a great deal from him.

The Star Trek Family

The official Star Trek account shared photos of Stewart as Picard and described a voice, a presence and a captain unlike any other, according to TV Insider. It said he gave generations of fans an icon who inspired them to look toward the stars.

William Shatner, who met Stewart on screen as Kirk and Picard in Star Trek Generations, called him a friend, a colleague and a magnificent actor in an Instagram post. He said Stewart brought intelligence, grace and humanity to everything he did, from the stage to the bridge of the Enterprise. George Takei, writing on Bluesky, said Stewart took the Enterprise to places it had never gone before and inspired a generation of stargazers to imagine a brighter future.

From The Next Generation, Jonathan Frakes posted a photo of Stewart with a short message of farewell, while Wil Wheaton shared a throwback picture captioned “Godspeed, Captain.” Terry Matalas, who ran the third and final season of Star Trek: Picard, paid his own tribute, per ABC News.

Friends From Comedy, Theatre and Home

Seth MacFarlane, who worked with Stewart on Family Guy, American Dad! and Ted, said Stewart could lift any line of dialogue and make a good writer sound great and a great writer sound masterful. He added that the loss would be felt around the world. Josh Gad wrote that Stewart was a man for all seasons, from Picard to the chess matches with Magneto to Shakespeare on the stage.

The Royal Shakespeare Company, where Stewart was a core member from 1966 to 1982, described him as a colossus of stage and screen and one of the finest classical Shakespearean actors of his generation. Closer to his roots, Huddersfield Town noted his Yorkshire pride and called him a Terrier in every sense, per Far Out Magazine. Sesame Workshop also marked his death, thanking him for sharing his kindness and joy.

A Career That Crossed Every Stage

Born on July 13, 1940, in Mirfield, West Riding of Yorkshire, Stewart trained at the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School and spent years in classical theatre before television made him a global name. He was knighted in 2010 for services to drama. His last major screen turn is still to come: he is set to appear as Xavier in Avengers: Doomsday, which reaches theaters on December 18.
