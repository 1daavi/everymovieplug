Chris Pratt Says Marvel Has Taken ‘Hard Action’ to Bring Star-Lord Back

By Maccarthy Albin

October 22, 2026

The actor says “he’s not going anywhere,” but won’t say when Peter Quill returns.

Chris Pratt says Star-Lord will return to the Marvel Cinematic Universe and that Marvel Studios has already taken “hard action” to make it happen, speaking on The Playlist’s Bingeworthy podcast.

The comments offer the most direct assurance yet from the actor about the future of Peter Quill, a character who has been absent from the franchise's main storyline since the close of the Guardians trilogy. They also arrive at a point when the MCU is preparing for a pair of large crossover films, which has encouraged fans to look closely at any hint about returning heroes.

What Pratt Said, and What He Left Out

Pratt said he has a good relationship with Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige and co-president Louis D’Esposito, and that conversations about delivering on the promise are ongoing. “You’ll see Star-Lord again,” he said, adding that he is not at liberty to say when. Marvel Studios declined to comment, according to TheWrap.

The statement is firm on the question of whether and vague on the question of when. Pratt did not name a film, a series or a year, and the studio has not confirmed any detail. That combination is typical of how Marvel handles returning characters, where actors are often asked to keep specifics private until an official announcement. The phrase “hard action” suggests that practical steps have been taken, but it does not reveal what those steps involved or how far along they are.

A Promise Written Into the Credits

The promise comes from the end of Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3 (2023), whose credits tag read “The Legendary Star-Lord Will Return.” The film left Peter Quill back on Earth, reconnecting with his grandfather, while Rocket took over leadership of a new Guardians team. Pratt has played the character in six films, from Guardians of the Galaxy through Thor: Love and Thunder.

A line in an end-credits scene carries particular weight in the MCU, since those sequences have long been used to signal what comes next. In this case the message was addressed to the character himself rather than to the franchise as a whole, which made it a clear statement of intent. Pratt's latest remarks suggest that the intention has not faded in the years since the film's release, and that the studio still sees a place for the character.

Pratt's Own Pitch

Earlier this year Pratt told the Happy Sad Confused podcast that he had pitched Feige his own idea for Star-Lord’s return and that he has a strong vision for the character.

An actor who develops his own concept for a role is a notable feature of the story. It indicates that Pratt is invested in the next chapter and is not simply waiting for a call. The details of the pitch have not been made public, and nothing in the available reports says whether Marvel intends to use it, but the fact that he raised it directly with Feige shows that the discussions have involved the studio's top leadership.

Why Star-Lord Still Matters to the MCU

The first Guardians of the Galaxy film arrived in 2014 and introduced a group of relatively obscure characters to a mass audience. Pratt, who was then best known for his comedic work on the television series Parks and Recreation, became a leading man through the role, and his career later expanded to include the Jurassic World films. Quill's mix of humor and sentiment made him a central figure in the MCU's cosmic stories, which is one reason that audiences have kept asking when he will come back.

The Road to Doomsday and Secret Wars

He has not been announced for Avengers: Doomsday, which opens December 18, though directors Joe and Anthony Russo have teased that more surprises are coming. Avengers: Secret Wars follows on December 17, 2027.

The Russo brothers directed the earlier Avengers films, including Infinity War and Endgame, and their return for these two entries has raised expectations about the size of the casts. The gap between the two releases is a year, which gives the studio room to bring figures into the story at different points. Since Pratt's role in those films has not been confirmed, any connection between his comments and the upcoming crossovers remains a matter of speculation.

Promoting a Different Role

Pratt made the comments while promoting season two of The Terminal List, which premiered on Prime Video on October 21.

The setting explains the tone of the conversation. Interviews tied to a current project often turn to a performer's other franchises, and Marvel is a subject that Pratt is asked about regularly. His remarks should be read in that light: they come from an actor discussing a promise made in a film several years ago rather than from a studio announcement, which is why they carry encouragement without concrete detail.
