Jeremy Allen White: The Next Wolverine?

By Maccarthy Albin

November 4, 2026 · 6:13 PM GMT

A report from Jeff Sneider says The Bear star Jeremy Allen White sat down with Marvel about a small part that could grow. Marvel has confirmed nothing.

Marvel Studios may be closer to finding its next Wolverine than anyone expected, and the name now circulating is one that fans have been floating for years. The Bear star Jeremy Allen White has reportedly met with Marvel about playing the clawed mutant in the studio’s upcoming X-Men reboot, according to industry insider Jeff Sneider.

The report arrives at a delicate moment for the franchise. Hugh Jackman has defined the character on screen for more than two decades, Marvel’s new X-Men team has been revealed without a Logan in sight, and the studio has said very little about how or when Wolverine will be recast. Nothing has been confirmed by Marvel, Disney or White’s representatives, but the rumor has already reignited one of the biggest casting debates in superhero cinema.

  • Jeff Sneider reports that Jeremy Allen White has met with Marvel about a role in the X-Men reboot
  • The part is described as small at first, with more screen time to follow
  • Marvel has not confirmed the meeting or announced a Wolverine
  • Marvel’s X-Men reboot is dated for May 5, 2028 and is directed by Jake Schreier

What the report actually says

The story began in Sneider’s InSneider newsletter, where the journalist wrote that there were whispers White had met with Marvel to discuss what is expected to be a small role in the upcoming reboot. According to that account, the part would lead to a larger presence down the line, which is how studios often introduce a new actor into a long-running universe.

Several outlets initially framed the item more strongly, with some headlines saying White was in talks to take over as Wolverine. Sneider himself clarified that he had reported a meeting rather than a negotiation. That distinction matters. A meeting can mean anything from a general conversation about the studio’s plans to a formal audition, and it does not mean an offer has been made or accepted.

Other commentators on social media have claimed the discussions are going well and that White is currently the studio’s leading option. Those claims are unverified and come from accounts that have no official connection to Marvel. The only firm detail is that the report points to a limited appearance first and a bigger role later, which would match the way Marvel has launched other characters before building them into leads.

A career that went from Shameless to The Bear

For readers who know White mainly from the kitchen of The Bear, the actor’s career is longer and more varied than that one role suggests. Born in Brooklyn in 1991, he began acting as a child in the theater and made his professional debut in 2006. His breakthrough came as Lip Gallagher on Shameless, the Showtime comedy-drama in which he appeared for a decade and in well over a hundred episodes between 2011 and 2021.

His career changed again in 2022 when he became the lead of The Bear, the FX series about a gifted chef who returns to Chicago to run his late brother’s sandwich shop. The performance earned him major awards recognition, including Emmys and a Golden Globe, and turned him into one of the most in-demand actors working in American television. He has since moved into film in a serious way, including a high-profile turn as Bruce Springsteen in Springsteen: Deliver Me From Nowhere, which placed him at the center of the 2025 awards conversation.

What stands out about that résumé is how often it leans on intensity. Lip Gallagher was a volatile, charming troublemaker. Carmen Berzatto in The Bear is an exhausted perfectionist who barely holds himself together. These are characters who carry anger, grief and talent in the same body, and that combination is exactly what Wolverine has meant to readers and audiences since the 1970s.

The meeting that has happened before

This is not the first time White’s name has been linked with Marvel. In 2023, he told GQ that he had already met with the studio and felt he handled the meeting badly, saying he “played it wrong.” The comment became part of the lore around his possible involvement, and some fans took it as evidence that the door had closed.

More recently, White was asked about the fan casting on Josh Horowitz’s Happy Sad Confused podcast. He did not say whether he would want the role, but he spoke warmly about Jackman’s work, saying the actor had done it for so long and was excellent. The answer was gracious and carefully neutral, and it neither confirmed nor ruled out anything.

Taken together, those moments suggest two things. First, Marvel has been aware of White for at least three years. Second, he appears to understand the weight that comes with the part. Any actor who steps into Wolverine’s boots is not simply taking a job but inheriting a performance that millions of people consider definitive.

Why the casting makes sense on paper

Several arguments are being made in White’s favor, and the most obvious is physical. In the comics, Wolverine is a short, stocky fighter, usually listed at around five feet three inches. Jackman, who stands well over six feet, was an unconventional fit when he was cast in 2000, and some purists complained for years that he was too tall for the role. White is reported to stand five feet seven inches, which is still taller than the comic version but far closer than any previous live-action Logan.

The second argument is dramatic range. Wolverine has always worked best when the character is more than a brawler. The strongest stories about him, from Logan to the best comics, are about a man who has lived too long, lost too much and still keeps choosing to protect people. White has built his reputation on playing damaged people with a short fuse and an unexpected tenderness.

The third argument is simple timing. White is in his mid-thirties, which puts him at roughly the age Jackman was when he began playing the role. That would give Marvel a long window to build stories around him, and the studio is clearly planning a long arc for its mutants.

There is also the Disney connection. The Bear is produced by FX and distributed through Hulu, both under the Disney umbrella, so the company already has an established relationship with White. For a studio that values continuity and control over its talent, that matters.

The case for caution

Not everyone is convinced, and the concerns are worth taking seriously. The first is the shadow of Jackman himself. Few actors have been so closely identified with a superhero. Jackman played Logan from 2000 through Logan in 2017, then returned for Deadpool & Wolverine in 2024, a film that grossed more than $1.3 billion worldwide and proved how much affection audiences still have for the character. Any successor will be compared with him from the first frame.

The second concern is timing. Some fans believe Marvel should not introduce Wolverine in the first X-Men movie at all, even in a cameo, because the character is so dominant that he could overshadow the new team. Others argue that a brief appearance is a smart way to let audiences get used to a new face before a larger story begins. Coverage of the rumor has shown a divided reaction, and that split is likely to continue until Marvel says something official.

The third concern is the nature of the reports themselves. Casting rumors are often early, incomplete or wrong. A meeting does not guarantee a role, and the entertainment industry is full of cases where the first name attached to a part never ended up on screen. Marvel has not commented, and until it does, the sensible reading is that White is one of several names the studio may be considering.

Where the X-Men reboot stands

The film that would introduce this new Wolverine is already taking shape. Marvel announced the project’s creative team and then unveiled its first cast at the D23 fan event in August, confirming a May 5, 2028 release date. Jake Schreier, who directed *Thunderbolts**, is behind the camera, and Michael Lesslie is writing the screenplay. Kevin Feige has described the film as very youth-oriented, which fits the ensemble Marvel has assembled so far.

The cast revealed so far includes Christopher Abbott as Professor X, Adam Driver as Mister Sinister, Sadie Sink as Jean Grey, Kit Connor as Cyclops, Samara Weaving as Emma Frost, Inde Navarrette as Rogue, Maya Boyd as Storm and Peter Claffey as Beast. The most notable absences are Wolverine, Magneto and Gambit, three characters who are central to the franchise’s identity. That gap is the reason the White report has attracted so much attention: it fills the most conspicuous hole in the lineup.

The film’s place in the larger Marvel calendar also matters. It follows Avengers: Secret Wars, which is scheduled for December 17, 2027, and Marvel has described that event as a reset that allows new versions of characters to arrive. In practical terms, that means the new X-Men will not simply continue the old Fox films but start fresh, which is why a recast Wolverine is expected.

The Jackman factor and Avengers: Doomsday

Before any of that happens, the old guard gets a send-off. Avengers: Doomsday, due in December 2026, will bring back Patrick Stewart, Ian McKellen and James Marsden, and Jackman is also expected to appear. The film is widely seen as the farewell to the Fox generation of mutants, a bridge between the franchise that began in 2000 and the one Marvel is building now.

That sequence helps explain why Marvel might want a new Wolverine to appear quietly and carefully. If Doomsday closes one chapter and Secret Wars resets the board, then a brief first appearance by a new Logan could connect the two eras without forcing audiences to choose between them. It would also give Marvel time to see how viewers respond before committing to a larger role.

There is a practical logic as well. Jackman has said for years that he cherishes the role and has returned to it more than once, but he is now in his late fifties, and a character that has been so central to the studio’s plans cannot be played by the same actor indefinitely. Recasting is coming, and the only question is who will be handed the claws.

What fans can expect next

For now, the next real news is likely to come from Marvel itself. Feige has said more casting announcements for the X-Men film are on the way, and the studio has a history of revealing key roles at events such as Comic-Con and D23 rather than through quiet press releases. If White is the choice, an official announcement could be timed to one of those moments.

Until then, the story is a rumor built on a report of a meeting, and it should be read as such. White has not commented on the latest item, Marvel has not confirmed it, and no contracts have been announced. What is clear is that the idea resonates: after years of fan art, social media campaigns and speculation, the actor many people have imagined as Logan is now being discussed by serious industry sources.
