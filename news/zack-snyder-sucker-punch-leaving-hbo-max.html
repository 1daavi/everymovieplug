Zack Snyder's Sucker Punch Is in Its Final Days on HBO Max

By Maccarthy Albin

October 11, 2026

The 2011 fantasy, Snyder's first original film, is about to be removed from the service.

Zack Snyder's Sucker Punch is about to leave HBO Max. Collider reports that the 110-minute fantasy is in its final days on the service, so anyone who wants to watch it there should do so soon.

The film was Snyder's first original project, following his Dawn of the Dead remake, 300 and Watchmen. It is his take on a young woman, Babydoll, who is committed to a psychiatric hospital and escapes through the shared fantasy worlds of the patients. Variety's ranking of Snyder's films calls it a bonkers tale.

The cast includes Emily Browning, Vanessa Hudgens, Jena Malone, Abbie Cornish, Oscar Isaac, Jon Hamm, Carla Gugino and Jamie Chung. Collider reports that it had a reported budget of $80 million and earned about $89 million worldwide, which made it the only Snyder film to lose money for Warner Bros.

Snyder has expressed interest in making one of his signature director's cuts of the film, but Collider says that remains purely conceptual.

When the Film Leaves

ComicBook.com reported on September 26 that Sucker Punch would leave HBO Max on October 31. Because that date has now arrived, availability may differ depending on the region and the day, and viewers should confirm on the service before planning a rewatch. The film has also been listed on Netflix, according to 3DVF, which noted in June that it was streaming on both Netflix and HBO Max.

A Film Billed as “Alice in Wonderland With Machine Guns”

ComicBook.com describes the marketing as promising Alice in Wonderland with machine guns, and notes that many viewers felt the finished film fell short of that pitch. The outlet says Babydoll is locked in an asylum and set to be lobotomized, which sends her into fantasy worlds where she and the other women are warriors looking for a way out. It praises the film’s visuals and action, saying it has a satisfying, video-game-like feel, while arguing that the story is where things came apart.

The film released in 2011 and later developed a cult following despite being a critical and box office failure, according to ComicBook.com. One of the most common criticisms concerned its portrayal of women and its use of sexual violence as a plot element. Snyder has said he was surprised the film was so misunderstood and has described it as a commentary on sexism and geek culture.

Theatrical Cut and Extended Cut

The theatrical version ran 110 minutes and carried a PG-13 rating. A Blu-ray extended cut added about 18 minutes and was rated R, according to Den of Geek. A library catalog entry for the home release lists the extended edition at 127 minutes, and other outlets give slightly different figures, so the exact running time varies by source. The home release also included special features such as a maximum movie mode and animated shorts.

The Original Ending and the Director’s Cut

Snyder recently said on the Happy, Sad, Confused podcast with Josh Horowitz that a director’s cut is in the works and that it will include new footage that has already been shot, according to ComicBook.com. No release timing has been given. The outlet adds that the new version could restore the ending Snyder originally wanted, in which Babydoll sings “Ooh, Child” on stage alongside the characters who were killed during the story. The studio considered that ending too weird and required a change.

Snyder also discussed the original ending in an interview with Letterboxd, reported by Variety and Cineserie, saying he wanted to use it for a planned director’s version. Reports on the cut have described it at different stages, with Collider calling it conceptual and ComicBook.com saying it is in the works, so Warner Bros. has not confirmed a release plan.

Box Office and Reputation

Collider puts the budget at $80 million and the worldwide gross at about $89 million, while 3DVF cites an $82 million budget and the same $89 million total. Either way, the film lost money for the studio. Its reputation has changed since then, and Netflix Junkie reported in November 2025 that the film was drawing renewed attention on HBO Max years after its release.

Who Is in the Cast

ComicBook.com credits Emily Browning, Jena Malone, Vanessa Hudgens, Jamie Chung and Oscar Isaac among the film’s stars, and Collider adds Abbie Cornish, Jon Hamm and Carla Gugino. The ensemble is built around Babydoll and the group of women who join her in the fantasy sequences.
