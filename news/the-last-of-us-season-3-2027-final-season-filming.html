The Last of Us Season 3 Is Set for 2027 and Is Planned as the Final Season

By Maccarthy Albin

October 26, 2026

Filming is scheduled to wrap November 27, with Kaitlyn Dever’s Abby at the center.

The Last of Us season 3 will arrive in 2027, HBO programming chief Casey Bloys told Deadline. “It’ll be happening in ’27. I don’t have a date,” Bloys said, adding, “I think Craig [Mazin] has planned it as the final season.”

The comments give fans their clearest timeline yet for the return of one of HBO's most closely followed dramas. They also introduce the possibility that the series is approaching its conclusion, a notable development for a show that has built its reputation on adapting a video game story with unusual care.

A Final Season, but Not Yet Official

Bloys stopped short of an official announcement that season 3 is the last, per Geektown. The season is expected to focus on Abby and cover the rest of The Last of Us Part II, which would bring the show level with the games.

The distinction between a planned ending and a confirmed one is important. Bloys framed the information as his understanding of what the show's creator intends, and he did not describe it as a network decision. In television, a creator's plan can shift as production unfolds, and networks sometimes leave room for additional seasons or spin-offs until a final episode has aired. For viewers, the safest reading is that season 3 is intended to bring the central story to a close, while HBO has not formally closed the door on anything beyond it.

The idea that the series would end when it catches up with the games is consistent with how the show has been structured. The source material consists of two main games, and an adaptation that covers both would have no further main story to draw on. That limit gives the producers a natural stopping point, and it explains why the plan would be to conclude rather than to continue indefinitely.

For context, the series premiered on HBO in January 2023 and adapts the 2013 game developed by Naughty Dog, with the sequel, The Last of Us Part II, following in 2020. That history explains why Bloys's remarks carry weight: the show has now moved from the first game into the territory of the second, and the final season would complete that journey.

Abby Moves to the Center

The focus on Abby marks a change in perspective for the series. Kaitlyn Dever returns in the role, and the season is expected to follow her side of events while Bella Ramsey, who plays Ellie, appears in a smaller role. For audiences, this means a season that asks viewers to spend more time with a character who was introduced as a source of conflict in the previous chapter.

Shifting the point of view in this way is a bold choice for a prestige drama. Many series keep their lead characters on screen throughout, and a season that deliberately reduces one of them in favor of another requires audiences to accept a new emotional center. The approach mirrors the structure of the second game, which asks players to experience events from more than one side, and it suggests that the producers are committed to preserving that design in the adaptation.

From Calm Current to a Late-November Wrap

Principal photography began in British Columbia on March 2 under the working title Calm Current and was scheduled to end November 27, according to Wikipedia. Production paused in June and resumed in late July. Craig Mazin is the sole showrunner and has directed episodes, per the same source.

A working title is a common practice on large productions, used to keep a project's identity low-profile while crews work on location. British Columbia's forests and coastlines provide a setting well suited to the overgrown, abandoned landscapes in which the story unfolds. A shoot running from early March to the end of November is a long one, and the pause in June and resumption in late July show that the schedule was not continuous. No reason for the break has been given, so any explanation would be speculation.

Mazin's status as the sole showrunner is a notable detail. He co-created the series with Neil Druckmann, the creative lead behind the original games, and now holds responsibility for the season's overall direction. His record of directing individual episodes places him close to the production on set as well as in the writers' room.

Returning Faces and New Arrivals

Kaitlyn Dever returns as Abby, Bella Ramsey returns as Ellie in a smaller role, and Jeffrey Wright returns as Isaac, per Wikipedia. Newcomers include Patrick Wilson as Jerry, Jason Ritter as Hanley, Peter Sarsgaard as Amon, Clea DuVall as a Seraphite and Li Jun Li as Miriam. Jorge Lendeborg Jr. replaced Danny Ramirez as Manny in February.

The list shows how the supporting ensemble is growing as the story moves into its second half. Several of the newcomers are established performers with long film and television credits, which signals that the production is investing in depth across the cast and not only in its lead roles. The recasting of Manny, with Lendeborg replacing Ramirez, is a reminder that even well-planned productions adjust their lineups as scheduling and availability change, and it was settled early enough in the process that the role is already accounted for.

The Sound of the Series

Gustavo Santaolalla is set to return to compose the score. Santaolalla scored the original games and has been closely associated with the series' sound, which relies on sparse, acoustic textures rather than conventional orchestral drama. His continued involvement offers a thread of continuity across the show and the games, and it suggests that the final season will keep the restrained musical identity that fans have come to expect.

What Remains Unannounced

HBO has not announced a premiere date or episode count.

With filming not scheduled to finish until late November, post-production will fill much of the time before release. Editing, sound design and visual effects work typically takes months on a series of this scale, and the 2027 window leaves room for that process. Until HBO shares a date, viewers can expect further updates on casting, trailers and the episode count to arrive over the coming months, and the question of whether the series ends with this season will likely be answered more clearly as the premiere approaches.
