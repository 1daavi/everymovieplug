Digger Opens With $8 Million in Fifth Place

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

Alejandro G. Iñárritu's original satire finished fifth in 3,321 theaters.

Digger, the Alejandro G. Iñárritu film starring Tom Cruise, earned about $8 million in its opening weekend, finishing fifth at the domestic box office. It played in 3,321 theaters, an average of roughly $2,409 per location.

Daily grosses declined across the weekend: about $3.4 million on Friday (including Thursday previews), $2.6 million on Saturday and $2.0 million on Sunday. On Tuesday, October 6, the film took in about $1.1 million, bringing its five-day total to roughly $9.9 million.

Cruise had been promoting the film on an international press tour. He appeared at a press conference in Seoul, South Korea, on October 1, the day before the film opened in the US.

Our full opinion piece on the film looks at why Digger may be remembered for more than its opening numbers.

An Original Satire With a Big Price Tag

Warner Bros. released Digger as a dark comedy about Digger Rockwell, a powerful oil tycoon played by Cruise under heavy makeup and a thick Southern accent. After his company's drilling sets off an environmental catastrophe, the character launches a frantic effort to present himself as humanity's savior. Iñárritu, the two-time Oscar winner behind Birdman and The Revenant, co-wrote the script with Sabina Berman, Nicolás Giacobone and Alexander Dinelaris Jr. The supporting cast includes Sandra Hüller, Riz Ahmed, John Goodman, Jesse Plemons, Michael Stuhlbarg, Sophie Wilde and Emma D'Arcy, and the film runs about two hours and nine minutes.

The project is also a rare departure for its star. Variety noted that it is Cruise's first film outside a franchise since 2017, and the studio sold it as an original event rather than a sequel. That approach carries a cost. Warner Bros. has described the budget as $125 million, although other reports place the figure higher, in a range between roughly $135 million and $180 million. Cruise is also reported to hold a first-dollar gross arrangement, which means he is paid from revenue before the studio recovers its costs.

International Numbers and Audience Response

Overseas markets added about $12 million, which brought the worldwide opening to roughly $20 million. For Cruise, the domestic figure is the lowest opening of his career since Lions for Lambs in 2007, although that earlier drama cost far less to make. Audiences gave Digger a C+ on CinemaScore, the same grade that Verity received that weekend, and it holds a Metacritic score of 45 out of 100. Rotten Tomatoes placed critics at roughly an even split, close to 50%.

Reviews have tended to praise the ambition, the scale of Cruise's transformation and the work of the supporting cast, while critics who disliked the film pointed to its length and its heavy-handed tone. One consistent theme across the coverage is that the film is a deliberate risk rather than a conventional crowd-pleaser, so audience reaction was always likely to be divided.

Where It Stands on the Weekend Chart

The opening placed behind Verity, which led the weekend with $32.6 million, along with Resident Evil, Heart of the Beast and Primetime. The film's per-theater average of about $2,409 was below those of its immediate competitors. The decline across the three days, from $3.4 million on Friday to $2.0 million on Sunday, is the pattern of a film that drew its core audience on opening day and found limited walk-up business afterward.

What Comes Next

Because the film cost so much relative to its opening, its theatrical path now depends on how well it holds in the coming weekends and on how much it earns abroad. A small second-weekend decline would help, but the starting point is low enough that reaching its production cost in theaters looks difficult. Supporters of the film hope that its craft and performances can find a second life through awards attention and later home viewing, which is a route that other divisive original films have taken in the past.

For now, the weekend's results show how difficult it is to open an expensive, non-franchise film, even with one of the biggest movie stars in the world attached. Cruise has publicly said he is proud of what the cast and crew made, and the studio will be watching the next few weekends to see whether word of mouth can improve the film's standing.
