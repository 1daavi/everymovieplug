Marvel's VisionQuest Premieres on Disney+ With Its First Two Episodes

By Maccarthy Albin

October 14, 2026

The series arrived a day early on October 13, and the full episode guide has been revealed.

Marvel’s synthezoid returned a day ahead of schedule. The first two episodes of VisionQuest are now streaming on Disney+. Marvel moved the premiere up a day, so the episodes arrived on Tuesday, October 13, at 6 p.m. PT (9 p.m. ET) instead of October 14, according to ComicBook.com and Laughing Place. The studio also published the titles of all eight episodes. Cast details, episode information and more are on our VisionQuest page.

The Release Schedule, Week by Week

Under the revised schedule, new episodes drop on Tuesdays rather than Wednesdays. The episodes are listed as follows:

  • October 13: Episode 1, “No Strings,” and Episode 2, “Theseus”
  • October 20: Episode 3, “Reboot,” and Episode 4, “Malware”
  • October 27: Episode 5, “Firewall,” and Episode 6, “Deadlock”

Laughing Place reports that the final two episodes will arrive individually, with Episode 7 due in November. Exact dates for those were not available in the reports reviewed.

Picking Up After WandaVision

The series follows the version of Vision who ended WandaVision as a white, reconstructed synthezoid without his memories. Official descriptions say he sets out to reclaim his identity. That ending, in which the character flew off to find out who he is, was left open on purpose, and the new show is built around that unfinished story. Paul Bettany returns as Vision, with James Spader back as Ultron, whom showrunner Terry Matalas has described as a core part of the show’s dynamic.

The cast also includes Emily Hampshire, James D’Arcy, Orla Brady and Ruaridh Mollica. D’Arcy and Brady play Tony Stark’s artificial assistants, J.A.R.V.I.S. and F.R.I.D.A.Y., which links the series to the broader history of Iron Man’s technology. Other announced cast members include Todd Stashwick as Paladin, a bounty hunter pursuing Vision, T’Nia Miller as Jocasta, Faran Tahir returning as Raza from the first Iron Man film, Mary McDonnell, and the comedian Diane Morgan.

The Showrunner and the Larger Picture

Terry Matalas serves as showrunner, and the eight-episode series is part of the MCU’s Phase Six. Matalas previously led the third season of Star Trek: Picard and the time-travel series 12 Monkeys, and Stashwick is among several actors who have worked with him in the past. It concludes the trilogy that began with WandaVision and continued with Agatha All Along, and it picks up one year after the events of WandaVision. Matalas has compared Stashwick’s character to Boba Fett, a description that suggests a pursuit story with a strong science-fiction flavor.

The trilogy framing gives the series a specific job. WandaVision introduced the idea of a reality shaped by grief, and Agatha All Along followed a different character through the aftermath of that story. VisionQuest closes the arc by returning to the figure at the center of the first show, so viewers who followed the earlier entries will find the most direct connections.

Mixed Notices From Critics

Early reviews are mixed to positive. VisionQuest held an 87% Fresh rating on Rotten Tomatoes as of Monday, according to Just Jared, though critics are divided. One review argued it might be the most passionless Marvel series, while ComingSoon rated it 7 out of 10.

The gap between a high aggregate score and a strongly negative individual review is a common pattern for Marvel television, where early critics often have access to only part of a season. Because Disney+ is releasing the show in two-episode batches for the first three weeks, opinions are likely to shift as more episodes become available, and the final verdict may depend heavily on how the last two installments land.

Machines, Sons and Old Enemies

Much of the interest in the cast comes from how many characters connect back to earlier chapters of the MCU. T’Nia Miller plays Jocasta, an android with a long history in Marvel Comics, where she is closely tied to Ultron. Ruaridh Mollica appears as Tommy Maximoff, one of the twin boys created in WandaVision, and pre-release coverage describes a father-and-son storyline alongside the chase. Faran Tahir’s return as Raza brings back a villain from the very first Iron Man film, linking the new series to the origins of the franchise.

Taken together, these returning figures give the show an unusual shape. It is a sequel to a story about grief, but it also revisits the technology of the Stark era, which Bettany’s character was built from. The characters of J.A.R.V.I.S. and F.R.I.D.A.Y. are part of that history, and their appearance in the cast list ties the series closely to the origins of Vision himself. Pre-release coverage has also pointed to a father-and-son thread between Vision and Tommy, which gives the premiere episodes an emotional center alongside the action.

What Viewers Should Know Before Watching

Anyone coming to VisionQuest fresh should plan on watching WandaVision first, since the new series begins from that show’s ending. Familiarity with Agatha All Along is helpful but less essential. The schedule makes it easy to keep pace: six episodes will be available by the end of October, and the remaining two will follow in separate weeks, which gives the show a long tail of conversation heading into November. Because the series concludes the trilogy, it is built to be watched as a complete story rather than as a standalone adventure.
