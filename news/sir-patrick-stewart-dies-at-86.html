Sir Patrick Stewart, Star of Star Trek and X-Men, Dies at 86

By Maccarthy Albin

October 29, 2026

The celebrated British actor, known as Captain Jean-Luc Picard and Professor Charles Xavier, died at his home in Los Angeles on October 28.

Sir Patrick Stewart, the British stage and screen actor who became a defining figure of science fiction as Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation and Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men films, has died at age 86. His family announced on Thursday, October 29, that he died the previous day at his home in Los Angeles, in the care of his wife, Sunny, after what the statement called “a brief and poignant journey” with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia, per Just Jared and Bloody Disgusting.

The Family’s Announcement

News of Stewart’s death spread quickly after the family released its statement, and it was soon carried by major news organizations in the United States and the United Kingdom. The statement described his final illness as brief and said he died at home with his wife beside him. It also served as a portrait of the man beyond his roles, listing his charitable concerns and the passions that defined his private life.

The announcement came on a Thursday, a day after his death on Wednesday, October 28. Stewart was 86.

From Mirfield to the Royal Shakespeare Company

Born in Mirfield, Yorkshire, in 1940, Stewart worked for more than 60 years across theatre, television and film, including 16 years with the Royal Shakespeare Company, according to the Press Association. That stage grounding shaped everything that followed. Audiences who knew him chiefly as a starship captain were often surprised to learn that his career began with classical drama, and that the commanding voice of Picard had been trained on Shakespeare.

He earned four Emmy nominations and two Olivier Awards for best supporting actor, the first for Antony and Cleopatra in 1979 and the second for Hamlet in 2009, per the same report. Wide Open Country adds that he won a Grammy in 1996 for a children’s spoken word album. The range of those honors, from the London stage to the recording studio, reflects a career that resisted easy categories.

Television Before the Bridge of the Enterprise

Before his breakthrough in genre roles, Stewart was known to audiences for 1970s television work including I, Claudius and Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy, per Far Out. Those credits placed him among the leading British dramatic actors of his generation well before American viewers met him.

He was later knighted by Queen Elizabeth II at Buckingham Palace, CBS News reported at the time. The honor recognized a career that had long since moved beyond any single role.

Captain Picard, on Screen for Decades

Stewart played Captain Jean-Luc Picard on Star Trek: The Next Generation and in the feature films Generations, First Contact, Insurrection and Nemesis, then returned to the role in the Paramount+ series Star Trek: Picard, according to Bloody Disgusting.

Picard became one of television’s most admired leaders, a thoughtful and diplomatic commander who favored debate over force. That characterization owed much to Stewart’s own gravity and warmth, and it helped broaden the franchise’s audience. Returning to the character decades later, in a new series, showed how closely viewers associated the role with him.

Professor X and the Marvel Connection

As Charles Xavier, he appeared in nine live-action X-Men films, Wide Open Country reports, including Logan. The role placed him at the center of a superhero series that treated its story as a drama about identity and acceptance, and his portrayal of a mentor who believes in peaceful coexistence gave the films a moral core.

He is also set to appear in Marvel Studios’ Avengers: Doomsday, which opens December 18. SciFiNow describes it as his final on-screen performance. The film will therefore carry added weight for audiences when it reaches theaters.

A Legacy Carried Into Marvel’s Next Chapter

Marvel’s upcoming slate offers a measure of how much the studio built around the characters Stewart helped define. Marvel Studios recently debuted about a minute of new Avengers: Doomsday footage in a World Series commercial, and it has announced the cast of its own X-Men film, which opens May 5, 2028, with Sadie Sink, Kit Connor and Adam Driver among those leading the roster. Related coverage on both projects appears below.

Those projects will introduce the mutant saga to new audiences, but the earlier films remain widely available, and they preserve Stewart’s version of Xavier for anyone who wants to revisit it. The same is true of Picard, whose adventures run across television series and feature films and give newcomers many places to begin.

Tributes From Colleagues and Studios

Marvel Studios said on X that Stewart gave Xavier “extraordinary grace, intelligence, and humanity,” and called him a generous collaborator and “a true gentleman,” per TV Insider. Jonathan Frakes, who played William Riker alongside Stewart and directed several Star Trek films, shared the family’s announcement on Instagram with a message wishing his friend rest in peace, TV Insider reports.

The tributes from two different franchises underline the unusual position Stewart held. Few actors become closely identified with a single beloved series, and fewer still do so with two. Colleagues and studios from both worlds have responded to his death with the same language of respect and gratitude.

Causes Beyond the Camera

The family’s statement also highlighted Stewart’s work on behalf of human rights, survivors of domestic violence, refugees, veterans’ mental health services and animal welfare, and described him as a Knight, Chancellor, motor sport enthusiast and lifelong Huddersfield football fan, per Just Jared.

That list of interests rounds out a picture of an actor who used his public standing to support causes he cared about. It also shows the personal side of a man who, for millions of viewers, existed mainly as a character on screen.

Survivors and Memorial Contributions

Stewart is survived by his wife, singer-songwriter Sunny Ozell, and two children from his first marriage, according to Laughing Place. In lieu of flowers, the family asked that memorial contributions go to the Johns Hopkins Alzheimer’s Disease Research Center Fund and the Alzheimer’s Foundation of America, per Just Jared.

The request ties his memory to research and support for the illnesses that marked his final period. Readers who wish to honor him in the way the family suggests can contribute to either organization.
