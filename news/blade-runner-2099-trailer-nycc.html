Blade Runner 2099 Releases Full Trailer and Sets November 25 Premiere

By Maccarthy Albin

October 11, 2026

The eight-episode series with Hunter Schafer and Michelle Yeoh is set 50 years after Blade Runner 2049.

The future of a famously bleak Los Angeles now has a release date. Prime Video unveiled the full trailer for Blade Runner 2099 at New York Comic Con on Saturday, October 10, and confirmed that the series will premiere on November 25. The eight-episode limited series was created by Silka Luisa and produced by Amazon MGM Studios and Alcon Television Group.

Fifty Years After 2049

The story is set 50 years after the events of Blade Runner 2049. Deadline’s description says Los Angeles has been reborn, but not by humanity. Hunter Schafer plays Cora, a fugitive who takes on one last identity as a Blade Runner. She is forced to partner with Olwen, a Replicant days from dying, played by Michelle Yeoh, and the two hunt a runaway who is hiding a truth that could collapse a fragile city. Dimitri Abold also stars, and Jonathan van Tulleken directed.

Early descriptions place Cora among the last surviving humans in a society now ruled by Replicants, a reversal that turns the franchise’s central question on its head. In the original story, humans hunted artificial beings who wanted to live. Here, the hunter is a human who has to live on terms set by her former creations.

What the Showrunner Said

At the panel, Luisa explained the premise by saying the replicant uprising hinted at in the films has taken place and the humans have lost, so the creations have surpassed their creators, according to Gizmodo. She said 2049 brought Rick Deckard’s story to a close, so it is time for new characters.

Luisa has also spoken about the opportunity to work with Ridley Scott, who directed the 1982 film and serves as an executive producer on the new series. She described sitting down with him to hear how the original movie was made as a once-in-a-lifetime experience. Michael Green, a writer on Blade Runner 2049, is also an executive producer, and she credited his knowledge of the universe and his skill at building the noir-style mystery that the franchise requires.

The Faces in the Trailer

Yeoh, an Oscar winner for Everything Everywhere All at Once, joined the project in May 2024, and Schafer, known for Euphoria, followed the next month. The wider cast listed by Prime Video includes:

  • Dimitri Abold, from Hijack and The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes
  • Lewis Gribben
  • Matthew Needham, from House of the Dragon
  • Katelyn Rose Downey
  • Tom Burke, from Furiosa
  • Maurizio Lombardi, from Ripley

Van Tulleken, whose credits include Shogun, directed the opening episodes and is also an executive producer. Luisa previously created the Apple TV+ mystery Shining Girls.

A Franchise With a Long Memory

The series is based on the world of the Philip K. Dick novel that inspired the 1982 film. That film, directed by Scott, drew on Dick’s 1968 novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? and became one of the most influential science-fiction films ever made, even though it was not an immediate success. Denis Villeneuve’s sequel, Blade Runner 2049, followed in 2017 with Ryan Gosling and Harrison Ford, and Scott was an executive producer on that film as well. Isa Dick Hackett, who represents the Dick estate, is among the executive producers of the new series.

The television project has been in development for several years. Scott first said in 2021 that a series was in the works, and Amazon announced the project in early 2022. A teaser appeared at San Diego Comic-Con in July, and the full trailer at New York Comic Con followed in October.

The Road to the Screen

The series changed hands behind the camera on the way to its premiere. A different director was attached to the opening episodes at one point, but scheduling conflicts forced him out by early 2024, and Van Tulleken took over the first two installments. Filming has since wrapped, which allowed Prime Video to move from teaser to full trailer to a firm premiere date within a few months. Karena Evans was also hired to direct episodes, according to reports, and the production drew a mix of film and television veterans to help realize a setting that audiences already know well from two feature films.

How to Watch

All eight episodes will arrive on November 25, exclusively on Prime Video in more than 240 countries and territories. Because the series is billed as a limited series, it is presented as a self-contained story rather than the first chapter of an open-ended run. The all-at-once release also means that viewers can choose their own pace, which suits a slow-burning mystery in a world built around mood and atmosphere.

For a franchise known for its visuals, the trailer offers a first look at how the production team imagines a city that has changed hands. The mix of neon, rain-soaked streets and a human protagonist who must rely on a dying Replicant suggests a story that keeps the genre’s noir roots while moving its themes forward, and it gives fans a firm date to plan around.
