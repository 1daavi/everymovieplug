Paramount Is Developing a Live-Action Cyberpunk 2077 Movie

By Maccarthy Albin

October 11, 2026

Transformers producer Lorenzo di Bonaventura is attached, with CD Projekt Red co-producing.

Paramount Pictures is developing a live-action film set in the world of Cyberpunk 2077, based on CD Projekt Red's best-selling role-playing game. Deadline broke the news on October 8. Lorenzo di Bonaventura, whose credits include the Transformers and G.I. Joe films, is attached to produce alongside CD Projekt Red, the Polish studio that owns the franchise.

The announcement brings one of the most recognizable science fiction games of recent years closer to the big screen, and it does so with the game's own developer involved in the production. At this stage the project is in development, and the details that have emerged so far concern its approach rather than its content.

A Story Set in Night City, Not a Retelling

Deadline reports the movie will tell a new story in the same universe rather than simply retelling the game. Dark Horizons notes it won't necessarily be a direct recreation of the game's plot. That is the approach Netflix took with the anime Cyberpunk: Edgerunners. No director, cast or release date has been announced, and it is unclear whether Keanu Reeves, who plays rocker Johnny Silverhand in the game, will appear.

Choosing an original story has practical advantages. The game is built around a long, branching narrative in which players make choices, and condensing that into a single film would require cutting heavily. A new plot lets the filmmakers draw on the setting, the factions and the tone without being bound to events that many fans have already experienced in different ways. It also leaves room for new characters, which is how Edgerunners found its own audience. The open question of Reeves's involvement is one that fans will likely follow closely, since his performance as Silverhand is among the game's best-known elements.

The World Behind the Game

The game is set in Night City, a megacity run by corporations such as Arasaka, where a mercenary called V is implanted with a chip carrying Johnny Silverhand's consciousness. Cyberpunk 2077 has sold more than 40 million copies worldwide, and its expansion Phantom Liberty has sold 15 million.

Those numbers explain Paramount's interest. A property with tens of millions of players brings a built-in audience, and the setting offers a ready-made visual identity of neon-lit streets, corporate towers and body modification. The game originated in the tabletop role-playing system created by Mike Pondsmith, and CD Projekt Red released it in December 2020 after years of anticipation. Its launch was marked by technical problems, but later updates and the success of Phantom Liberty helped restore its standing. Together with Edgerunners, which arrived in 2022, they turned a troubled release into a franchise with considerable momentum.

How Edgerunners Set the Template

Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, produced by the Japanese animation studio Trigger and released on Netflix in 2022, told a self-contained story about a young man in Night City and drew praise from critics and players alike. It showed that the universe could support narratives that stand apart from the game, and it brought new viewers to the franchise at a moment when the game itself was still recovering from its rocky start. The Paramount film appears to follow the same logic, offering a fresh story that rewards players while remaining open to people who have never touched the controller.

Who Is Making It

Di Bonaventura's background in large-scale franchise filmmaking is the main clue about how the studio sees the project. His record includes the Transformers and G.I. Joe series, which means he has experience bringing established properties to a global audience. Pairing him with CD Projekt Red, the Polish studio that owns the franchise, suggests that the developer will keep a hand in shaping how its world appears on screen. Many recent game adaptations have involved the original creators in this way, a practice intended to protect the tone and details that fans value.

Paramount's Growing Game Slate

The film is Paramount's third major video game adaptation in development. Street Fighter, which the studio is co-producing with Legendary, opens on October 16, and a Call of Duty film directed by Peter Berg is set for 2028. Gizmodo credits Sony's Resident Evil for helping make game adaptations popular again, and you can read about its box office run in our holdovers report.

The pattern is clear. Studios have been looking to video games as a source of established characters and worlds, and the commercial performance of recent adaptations has encouraged them. A slate that includes a fighting game, a military shooter and a science fiction role-playing game shows Paramount drawing from a range of genres rather than committing to a single style. Each film will face the challenge of satisfying fans of the source material while also appealing to audiences who have never played the games.

A Studio in the Middle of a Larger Story

Paramount is also in the middle of a much bigger story, as it completed its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery this week. You can read about that in our report on the merger.

The timing places the Cyberpunk 2077 announcement within a period of significant change at the company. A merger of that scale reshapes how a studio plans its slate, and a project that begins development in the middle of such a transition will be watched for signs of how the combined company intends to operate. For now, the film is one of several projects in the early stages.

What Remains Unknown

No director, cast, script or release date has been announced, and the project is still at the development stage, where plans can change or stall. Fans of the game will likely have to wait for further reports before learning who will bring Night City to life, what story the film will tell and whether the character of Johnny Silverhand will appear.

Projects in development can take years to reach theaters, and some never do, so the announcement is best understood as a first step. Paramount's other game adaptations will offer early clues about how the studio approaches the genre. Street Fighter arrives first, on October 16, and its reception may influence how audiences and investors view the studio's wider plans for the category. Until more details emerge, the Cyberpunk 2077 film remains an intriguing prospect rather than a scheduled release.
