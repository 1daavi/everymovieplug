Lanterns Season 2 Is Not Greenlit Yet, and DC Promises an Answer by Year's End

By Maccarthy Albin

October 15, 2026

Updated October 28, 2026

HBO has not ordered more episodes, even after a record-setting finale, but DC Studios co-head Peter Safran says fans will learn the show's fate soon.

HBO has not greenlit a second season of Lanterns. According to Cosmic Book News, DC Studios co-head Peter Safran said on October 14 that fans will learn the show's fate before the end of the year.

A Finale That Broke the Show's Own Records

The first season premiered on August 16, and its finale aired on October 4. The finale drew 10.5 million viewers worldwide over its first three days on HBO and HBO Max, a series high, according to Deadline's report as relayed by Cosmic Book News. In the US it reached 7.2 million viewers, also a series best.

The season-long picture is just as strong by HBO's own account. The network says the season averaged 19.3 million viewers per episode worldwide, which it counts among its five biggest series launches, and that the premiere beat the openings of IT: Welcome to Derry, The Penguin and True Detective: Night Country. Separately, Warner Bros. Discovery said the series drew 9.3 million global viewers in its first three days, as reported by Cosmic Book News. Screen Rant notes that the show holds a 91% score on Rotten Tomatoes.

A rising finale audience is notable in its own right. Series often peak at the premiere and taper through the season, so a closing episode that sets a high-water mark suggests viewers stayed with the story, or arrived late through catch-up viewing. Together with a strong critic score, the numbers make a reasonable case that the show found an audience, which is why the absence of a renewal has drawn so much attention.

What the HBO Brass Has Said So Far

HBO chief Casey Bloys said before the finale that no Lanterns Season 2 was in development, and that HBO is very selective about continuing its DC shows. He was asked at the network's Emmy party in September whether anything else was active at DC, and answered that nothing was in development right now apart from The People v. Gorilla Grodd, a Jimmy Olsen mockumentary that has started shooting in Atlanta. He did not directly address a Lanterns follow-up.

James Gunn confirmed the October 4 episode was a season finale, not a series finale, but did not say a second season was coming. After the finale, Cosmic Book News reported that Deadline says a renewal is pending at Skydance's HBO. Taken together, the statements leave the situation open: nobody has ruled out more episodes, and nobody has announced them.

Safran's promise of an answer by the end of the year gives the wait a boundary. It does not say which way the decision will go, but it suggests the studio does not intend to leave the question unresolved into next year.

A Possible Second Season Taking Shape on Spec

Showrunner Chris Mundy told The Hollywood Reporter in August that he and Christopher Cantwell were developing a possible second season on spec, with no scripts written at that point. By October 5 he said there was nothing official to share. Screen Rant reports that a writers' room has reportedly been opened, and that Skydance's acquisition of Warner Bros. could be complicating or delaying an announcement, though that has not been confirmed. You can read about the deal in our report on the merger.

Developing a season on spec means the writers are shaping ideas without a formal order behind them. It is a way of staying ready, and it is common when a creative team believes a show has a future but the business decision has not yet been made. The reported writers' room would fit that pattern, though the report remains unconfirmed.

The corporate backdrop adds uncertainty. A change in ownership often means a review of existing commitments and priorities, and decisions about continuing a series can wait while new leadership settles in. That explanation is reported as a possibility rather than a fact, and no one involved has confirmed it.

Gunn's Distance and the Fate of Hal Jordan

Mundy also said James Gunn was busy with Superman when the show started and mostly gave high-level notes, trusting the writers with the story. The detail clarifies how the series was made. Although it sits inside the larger DC Studios slate overseen by Gunn and Safran, much of its creative direction came from the writers and the production team rather than from constant oversight at the top.

He added that the death of Hal Jordan was planned from the start, and that Kyle Chandler knew about it before filming. That choice places the first season's conclusion in a different light, because it means a major character's exit was a built-in feature of the story and not a late reaction to circumstances.

Chandler, who plays Hal Jordan, and Aaron Pierre, who plays John Stewart, have both said they are at peace if there is no second season. The comments are measured rather than pessimistic. They suggest the actors view the first season as a complete story, whatever the studio ultimately decides.

Two Creators Already Moving On

Co-creators Damon Lindelof and Tom King will not be part of a second season, because both are working on other series. Their absence means any continuation would rely on Mundy and the writers who developed the follow-up on spec, and would carry the show forward under a somewhat different creative leadership than the one that launched it.

For another measure of the show's reception, see our report that Lanterns has the lowest audience score of any DCU project. That figure sits uneasily beside the 91% critics' score and the record viewership, and it shows that opinion about the series is not uniform.

Mixed Signals Before the Deadline

The available evidence points in several directions at once. Viewership grew into the finale and HBO calls the launch one of its biggest. Critics have responded warmly. Against that, Bloys has described a selective approach to DC series, the audience score is the lowest in the DCU, and the ownership change may be slowing decisions. None of these factors settles the question alone, and the studio has not revealed how it weighs them.

Until Safran's promised announcement arrives, the status of Lanterns is straightforward: season one is complete, a second season is not ordered, and no one involved has announced anything official. Fans will have an answer, one way or the other, before the year is out.
