Marvel Reveals New Avengers: Doomsday Footage in a World Series Commercial

By Maccarthy Albin

October 24, 2026

The one-minute spot puts the Fantastic Four at Ebbets Field, features real MLB players and ends on Doctor Doom.

Marvel Studios revealed new Avengers: Doomsday footage in a special commercial during Game 1 of the 2026 World Series on Friday, October 23. The roughly one-minute spot shows the Fantastic Four at Ebbets Field and ends with Doctor Doom, according to MLB.com.

A First Pitch From Reed Richards

MLB.com reports that the promo shows a scene in which Reed Richards throws a ceremonial first pitch, with Doctor Doom appearing in the sky at the end. Fantasy Land News describes the setting as Ebbets Field on the Fantastic Four’s home world, Earth-828, with a Dodgers “World Champs” banner on the stadium and fans holding a sign reading “Four Heroes, One Historic Field.”

The choice of location carries some history. Ebbets Field was the Brooklyn home of the Dodgers until the team moved to Los Angeles after the 1957 season, and the ballpark was demolished a few years later. Placing the Fantastic Four there fits the retro-futuristic version of New York seen in The Fantastic Four: First Steps, which was set in an alternate 1960s and introduced the team to the Marvel Cinematic Universe in 2025. It also lets Marvel tie a piece of baseball nostalgia to a superhero story at the exact moment the sport’s biggest series was on television.

Players in the Dugout

Real players appear in the dugout in their own uniforms: Bryce Harper, Freddie Freeman, Randy Arozarena, Junior Caminero and Jacob Misiorowski, who started Game 1 for the Brewers, per MLB.com and Fantasy Land News.

The list is notable for its range. It includes established stars and younger names, and it spans several clubs, not only the two teams playing that night. For a commercial built to run during a championship broadcast, that mix gives viewers from many fan bases a reason to recognize someone on screen. It also marks the spot as a collaboration with the league and not just an ad placed around the game.

What Else Appears in the Footage

Fantasy Land News also lists Ant-Man and Sam Wilson in his Captain America suit, Thor calling down lightning and Black Panther entering the frame. The footage ends on Doctor Doom in his mask and hood, followed by a closing card reading “12.18.26 Is Doomsday.”

That line-up is the more important detail for fans tracking the film’s scope. A single minute of footage that touches the Fantastic Four, the Avengers and Wakanda’s hero suggests a story that brings multiple corners of the franchise together. The spot does not show dialogue-heavy scenes or plot specifics, and nothing in the reporting describes how these characters meet or how their worlds connect. What it does establish is tone: a ballpark celebration interrupted by a threat from above.

Doctor Doom Takes the Final Frame

Ending on Doctor Doom is a deliberate choice. Marvel has been building toward the character as the central antagonist of the Avengers films directed by Anthony and Joe Russo, and the villain was announced as being played by Robert Downey Jr. at San Diego Comic-Con in 2024. Showing Doom as the last image, instead of the heroes, tells viewers where the film’s focus lies.

Doom’s arrival over a sports stadium also has a visual logic. The scene works as a contrast between a crowd gathered for a ceremony and an enemy who appears without warning, which gives a short promo a clear beginning, middle and end without requiring any knowledge of the larger plot.

How the Preview Was Announced

MLB announced the preview a day earlier with a video reading, “Something’s coming… Tune in to World Series Game 1 for an exclusive preview of Avengers: Doomsday,” according to ComicBook.com. Game 1 between the Tampa Bay Rays and the Milwaukee Brewers aired on Fox at 8 PM.

Announcing the exclusive a day ahead gave fans a reason to watch live rather than wait for clips online. Premiering marketing material during a major sports broadcast is a familiar strategy for studios that want a large, simultaneous audience, and the World Series offers exactly that.

Why a Baseball Broadcast?

Studios have increasingly looked to live events when they want attention that cannot be skipped or delayed. A championship game draws viewers who are tuned in from the first pitch to the final out, and commercial breaks are part of the viewing experience. For Marvel, which is promoting a film opening in December, a late-October placement lands roughly seven weeks before release, early enough to build anticipation without revealing the full story.

The tie-in also fits the film’s own imagery. A baseball setting let the commercial speak directly to the audience watching, instead of simply reusing material from a trailer. Whether this approach points to a larger campaign pattern has not been addressed by the studio in the reporting available.

What Fans Should Keep in Mind

Short promotional spots like this one are built to generate conversation, and details can be easy to over-read. The reporting agrees on the central points: the Ebbets Field setting, the first pitch from Reed Richards, the appearance of real players and the final shot of Doctor Doom. Other specifics, such as the exact order of cameos and the wording of signs in the crowd, come from individual outlets’ descriptions and are best confirmed by watching the footage directly.

For readers following the road to December, the practical takeaway is simple. Marvel has now shown a sliver of the film in a high-profile slot, confirmed that the Fantastic Four are part of it, and put its villain in the final frame. More footage, a full trailer or additional cast announcements could follow in the weeks ahead, but none has been scheduled in the sources cited here.

The Release Date

Avengers: Doomsday opens in theaters on December 18, 2026.

Details beyond the footage itself, such as plot, the full cast list and the structure of the story, have not been confirmed in the sources used for this report. Marvel has not said whether the ballpark scene appears in the finished film in the same form as in the commercial, so viewers should treat the spot as a promotional piece until the movie arrives.
