Is Batman in Clayface? What Is Known as the Film Opens

By Maccarthy Albin

October 23, 2026

A leak from an early screening says no, and the director has said the story belongs to Matt Hagen.

Clayface opens in theaters today, and the question hanging over it is whether Batman shows up. A leak from an early screening says he does not.

What the Leak Claims

ComicBookMovie reported on October 18 that an anonymous poster on r/DC_Cinematic, running an AMA after seeing the film, said they “got no sense of Batman or even Bruce Wayne’s presence.” The same leak reportedly says there are no wider DCU references, no other Batman villains and no tie-in to Creature Commandos.

A caution applies. The claim comes from an anonymous poster on a forum, and it has not been confirmed by Warner Bros. or by DC Studios. Leaks of this kind can be accurate, incomplete or mistaken, and a single viewer’s account may miss a brief moment or a post-credits scene. The report is best read as an indication of what early viewers are saying, not as a final statement about the contents of the finished film.

The Director’s Position

Director James Watkins left the door slightly open in July. Asked about a Batman cameo, he told ComicBookMovie, “The story is Clayface’s story, it’s Matt Hagen’s story,” and added, “You don’t necessarily bump into any of these people… It’s just one big city.”

Watkins’s answer neither confirms nor rules out an appearance. What it does show is a filmmaker emphasizing the focus of the story on its title character. His comment about a single large city suggests that Gotham is treated as a shared setting in which characters do not automatically cross paths. Watkins previously directed the 2012 horror film The Woman in Black, and the tone of his earlier work is consistent with a character study that leans toward the darker side of the genre.

Early Reviews

Rotten Tomatoes' first-reviews roundup shows the film at 75% and Certified Fresh. It quotes Frank Scheck of The Hollywood Reporter calling Clayface “one of the year’s big surprises,” and Josh Wilding of ComicBookMovie.com calling it “Morbius, but slightly better.”

The two reactions point in different directions, and that spread says something about the response. A Certified Fresh designation means the early critical consensus is favorable, and Scheck’s remark is strongly positive. Wilding’s comparison, on the other hand, is a more reserved judgment, since Morbius is a 2022 comic book film that was widely criticized. A score from a first-reviews roundup also reflects a limited number of early critics and can shift as more reviews are published.

Who Is Matt Hagen?

Clayface is a long-running Batman villain, and several characters have carried the name in the comics. Basil Karlo was the first, debuting in 1940, and Matt Hagen was introduced later as a new version of the character. Because the film centers on Hagen, it follows the version of the character who is typically depicted as a man who gains shape-shifting abilities, with the details of how the film handles that origin left to the film itself.

Why the Batman Question Keeps Coming Up

Batman is the best-known character associated with Gotham City, so any film set in that world raises the question of whether he will appear. Audiences have seen the character in many films across several decades, and the expectation of a cameo has become part of how superhero spin-offs are discussed before release. For Clayface, that expectation is heightened by the film’s place in a new shared universe, where connections between titles are closely watched. The reports gathered here suggest that this film chooses to stay with its own characters, and that choice is part of what the director describes as the story of Matt Hagen.

Cast, Crew and Release

Tom Rhys Harries stars as Matt Hagen, with Naomi Ackie as Dr. Caitlin Corr and Max Minghella as a Gotham City detective, according to Wikipedia. Mike Flanagan and Hossein Amini wrote the screenplay. The Warner Bros. release was moved from September 11 to October 23 for the Halloween window, and Wikipedia lists a budget of about $40 million. Wikipedia also describes the film as part of the DCU’s Chapter One: Gods and Monsters.

The writing team combines a filmmaker known for horror storytelling, Flanagan, with Amini, whose credits include the crime drama Drive. The release date change places the film in the weeks before Halloween, which fits a story about a monstrous character. A budget of about $40 million is modest by superhero film standards, and the figure comes from Wikipedia and is not a studio statement.

What Viewers Should Take Away

As of the film’s opening, the evidence on Batman is limited to an unconfirmed leak and a director’s careful comments. Viewers who go in hoping for a cameo should know that early reports point the other way, while those who want a standalone story about Hagen may find that the description matches what they hope to see. Whether the film connects to the wider DCU in any visible way is also a matter of reports and not of confirmed fact. The most reliable answer will come from watching the finished film in theaters.
