Sinners Music Exhibition Opens at California African American Museum

By Maccarthy Albin

October 20, 2026

The show, built around Sammie’s guitar and the Club Juke sequence, runs through March 14.

An exhibition dedicated to the music of Ryan Coogler’s Oscar-winning Sinners opened October 20 at the California African American Museum (CAAM) in Los Angeles. Sinners: Sounds That Summon runs through March 14, according to Variety.

CAAM and Warner Bros. Archives co-organized the show, which was curated by Imani Y. Haynes. Variety describes it as the first museum exhibition devoted to a film’s music.

The exhibition is centered on the guitar used by protagonist Sammie ‘Preacher Boy’ Moore. It revisits the Club Juke sequence, in which the music links traditional African ritual to present-day street dance. Visitors will also see costumes, props and archival material, including Smoke and Stack’s costumes and the Club Juke sign.

The show ends with Sounds That Carry Us, a participatory experience based on the contrast between the joy and safety inside Club Juke and the danger outside it. CAAM’s official description frames the show around how Black music carries memory, movement, style and spirit across generations.

Three Themes Behind the Title

The exhibition explores its title through three themes: Music Makes Sounds, Music Makes Movement, and Music Makes Worlds, according to Fangoria. The three headings frame sound, movement and world-building as the exhibition’s main ideas. The centerpiece is the guitar played by Miles Caton, who portrays Sammie in the film, displayed alongside the costumes worn by Michael B. Jordan as the twin brothers Smoke and Stack.

CAAM describes the show as an exploration of how sound can form creative worlds that manifest joy, protection, belonging and freedom. Its focus on Club Juke, the fan-favorite sequence from Coogler’s 2025 film, gives the exhibition a single dramatic scene to build around. The film was made by Coogler and Proximity Media and released by Warner Bros.

Time Out calls the show an immersive presentation that gives fans an in-depth look at the world-building of Coogler’s film, and it identifies the Club Juke sequence as the exhibition’s centerpiece. Combining original props, costumes and production materials from the Oscar-winning film, as EURweb describes them, the display lets visitors see up close the objects that appeared on screen during the film’s most talked-about scene.

From the Mississippi Delta to California

The exhibition goes beyond the film itself. Time Out reports that it also highlights the historical journey of the Great Migration, showing how Black music and culture traveled from the Mississippi Delta to California and planted seeds for new forms of artistic expression. That framing connects the story on screen, which is set in the Mississippi Delta, to the Los Angeles audience visiting the museum.

The film is also the subject of a house at this year’s Halloween Horror Nights, according to Fangoria, and it earned awards at the 2026 Oscars, so the museum show arrives as part of a wider run of attention for the film.

Sounds That Carry Us

The final installation invites visitors to take part rather than only look. According to EURweb, the interactive piece encourages people to reflect on songs and sounds associated with joy, hope and community, and visitors can contribute their own musical memories, making the exhibition a shared celebration of Black cultural expression.

The Curator’s Perspective

Haynes is an independent curator, according to the exhibition listing on ArtRabbit. TheGrio reported that when she first walked into the finished exhibition at CAAM, the sight of it moved her to tears. The outlet’s feature describes her aim as inviting visitors to feel Coogler’s juke joint scene again, this time inside a gallery.

A Museum With a Musical History

CAAM has presented music-focused shows before. In 2018 it opened How Sweet the Sound, an exhibition on gospel music in Los Angeles developed in collaboration with the University of Southern California Gospel Music Archive, according to the museum’s press materials. Sinners: Sounds That Summon continues that tradition by placing a contemporary film alongside the long history of Black musical expression in the city.

Visitor Information

The exhibition is on view from October 20, 2026, through March 14, 2027, and admission is free, per EURweb. The museum is in the USC and Exposition Park area of Los Angeles, according to Time Out. ArtRabbit lists regular hours of 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Tuesday through Saturday and 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Sunday, with the museum closed on Mondays. Visitors can check the California African American Museum’s official exhibition page for current details before planning a trip.
