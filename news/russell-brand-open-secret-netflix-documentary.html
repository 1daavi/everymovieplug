Netflix’s Russell Brand: Open Secret Arrives Days After His 10½-Year Prison Sentence

By Biswajit Das

November 12, 2026

The 84-minute documentary traces the comedian’s rise and fall and premieres on November 13. The 2010 comedy Get Him to the Greek links his story to Sean “Diddy” Combs, who is serving a federal sentence of his own.

Netflix will release Russell Brand: Open Secret, a documentary about the comedian’s career and the criminal case that ended it, on Friday, November 13. The film arrives just days after a London court sentenced Brand to 10½ years in prison. A jury at Southwark Crown Court found him guilty last week of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, and Brand, who denied every charge, was sentenced on Monday, November 9. Directed by Olivia Isaacs and produced by 72 Films, the same company behind Netflix’s Jimmy Savile: A British Horror Story, the documentary uses archive footage and interviews with insiders to follow Brand from comedian and film star to political activist and YouTuber, and finally to the dock.

This is a serious subject, and the facts are laid out below in the order the reporting supports them: what Netflix has said about the film, what the court decided, how the streamer has handled Brand’s older material, and why his name has been tied this week to another Netflix subject, Sean “Diddy” Combs, through the 2010 film Get Him to the Greek. Where a claim comes from an anonymous source, we say so. If you or someone you know has been affected by sexual violence, organizations such as RAINN in the United States and Rape Crisis England & Wales offer confidential support.

  • Russell Brand: Open Secret premieres on Netflix on November 13, 2026, with a runtime of 84 minutes
  • Brand was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault and sentenced to 10½ years on November 9; he denied all charges
  • The film features The Times and Sunday Times reporters Rosamund Urwin and Charlotte Wace, whose 2023 investigation led to the case
  • Netflix has stopped promoting Brand’s 2018 special Re:Birth but has not removed it
  • An anonymous source told a gossip newsletter that Combs, Brand’s Get Him to the Greek co-star, plans to contact him; the claim is unconfirmed

What Netflix says the documentary covers

According to Netflix’s own Tudum site, as reported by Rolling Stone and HuffPost UK, Open Secret “charts the story” of Brand’s meteoric rise and steep fall after allegations of sexual offenses. It promises inside voices and archive footage that give insight into his life and into the investigation by The Times and The Sunday Times, led by chief media correspondent Rosamund Urwin and investigations correspondent Charlotte Wace. A short teaser, according to Rolling Stone, calls Brand a shapeshifter and a grifter and says the journalists did not initially realize their reporting would lead to criminal charges. Netflix describes his path as running from comedian and film star to political activist and YouTuber known for conspiracy theories, ending in a headline-dominating trial.

Isaacs previously directed the true-crime series The Other Mrs Jordan. 72 Films is part of Fremantle, and What’s on Netflix lists the film at 84 minutes. Deadline reported after the verdict that Netflix was close to launching a Brand project and that the streamer declined to comment, and it noted that a former friend of Brand’s told the trial he had been approached by a producer about a Netflix show. The premiere date was announced on November 12, a day before release. The coverage reviewed for this article does not include a response from Brand to the documentary.

The verdict and the sentence

The legal outcome is the foundation of the film, so the details matter. The case began in September 2023, when The Sunday Times, The Times and Channel 4 published a joint investigation into allegations from several women. London’s Metropolitan Police began investigating that month, and the Crown Prosecution Service authorized charges in April 2025. Brand pleaded not guilty at Southwark Crown Court in May 2025. At the trial, six women testified about episodes they said took place between 1999 and 2009. Brand told the court that the events had not happened or that the women were reinterpreting consensual encounters, according to the report carried by WRAL.

On Thursday, November 5, a jury found Brand guilty of one count of rape, concerning an encounter in 2009, and three counts of sexual assault. He was found not guilty on two other counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. On Monday, Judge Joel Bennathan sentenced him to 10 years and six months, which Deadline reports was made up of nine years for the rape and 18 months for the sexual assaults. The judge described Brand as a “sexual predator” who showed arrogant disregard for women’s wishes, according to the report carried by WRAL. NBC News reported on Tuesday that British detectives were assessing a number of new reports about Brand following the sentencing, including two other allegations of rape, one involving a 16-year-old girl. No new charges have been announced.

Netflix, Re:Birth and the backlash

The documentary lands in the middle of a dispute over how Netflix treats Brand’s earlier work. His 2018 stand-up special Russell Brand: Re:Birth has been on the service since December 2018. Following the verdict, Netflix declined to remove it but, according to Deadline, no longer actively promotes it: it does not appear in viewing recommendations or when members search for Brand’s name. Yahoo News UK reported that the decision to keep the special online drew backlash. The result is an unusual situation in which Netflix hosts a Brand comedy hour that it has quietly de-emphasized, and, from Friday, a documentary about his crimes that it is promoting heavily.

The timing also shows how quickly streaming services can turn real events into programming: the premiere follows the sentence by four days. Netflix has leaned on true-crime documentaries before, and Every Movie Plug’s coverage of the streamer’s current slate, from Netflix Unveils The Take First Look With Denzel Washington and Robert Pattinson to Jason Statham and Guy Ritchie Reunite for Netflix Revenge Thriller Dogfight, shows how much of its calendar is built around scripted stars. True-crime documentaries fill a different role: they are fast, relatively inexpensive and tied to the news cycle.

The Get Him to the Greek connection

The link between Brand and Sean “Diddy” Combs runs through a film. In Get Him to the Greek, the 2010 comedy directed by Nicholas Stoller, Brand played the rock star Aldous Snow, a character he first played in Forgetting Sarah Marshall, opposite Jonah Hill as the record-label employee assigned to deliver him to a concert. Combs had a prominent supporting role as Sergio Roma, the head of the label that employs them. The backdrop below shows Combs in the film.

Both men are now incarcerated, in unrelated cases. Combs was convicted in July 2025 on two counts of transportation for purposes of prostitution and acquitted of racketeering conspiracy and sex trafficking charges. He was sentenced in October 2025 to 50 months in federal prison. He was recently moved from FCI Fort Dix to FCI Allenwood Low in Pennsylvania, according to Page Six, and coverage of the federal inmate locator has shown a projected release date in early 2028 that has shifted more than once. His lawyers are awaiting a decision on his appeal, and no pardon has been reported.

The latest overlap is a report. On November 10, gossip journalist Rob Shuter wrote in his Naughty But Nice Substack that an unnamed source said Combs has a lot of sympathy for Brand, remembers their Hollywood days and plans to contact him. The item was picked up by several outlets, including Yahoo, which attributed it to the same source. Neither man’s representatives have been quoted confirming it in the coverage reviewed here, and an anonymous claim in a gossip newsletter should be treated as unverified. What can be said is limited: the two co-starred in one film in 2010, and that credit is the connection. A shared film credit does not link their cases, and nothing in the reporting suggests the two are connected in any criminal matter.

What the two stories do share is a streaming angle. Netflix released Sean Combs: The Reckoning, a four-part documentary series executive produced by Curtis “50 Cent” Jackson and directed by Alexandria Stapleton, on December 2, 2025. Combs’s representatives called it a hit piece and said the team was considering legal options over unauthorized footage, according to Deadline. Netflix’s description of the series calls Combs a convicted offender and notes that he is appealing. With Open Secret, the streamer now has documentary treatments of two men who appeared together in a mainstream studio comedy, a link that comes from casting history and not from any shared case.

Real events, screen versions and the people involved

Documentaries about living or recently convicted people raise a question that dramas have faced for years: how much of a screen version can the subject accept? Every Movie Plug’s look at American Gangster describes how the real people portrayed in that film disputed it, and The Social Network raised the same argument about the founding of Facebook. A documentary has a different burden. It relies on testimony, court records and archive footage rather than invented scenes, and in this case it rests on a verdict reached by a jury and a sentence handed down by a judge.

The film also puts journalists at the center. The investigation by Urwin and Wace is part of the story, and the news business has its own screen history, including the dark portrait of a freelance news-gatherer in Today in Cinema, October 31: Nightcrawler Opens on Halloween 2014. The judge himself said in his sentencing remarks, according to Yahoo Style Canada, that Brand’s status had protected him until journalists collected and published the allegations in 2023.

Netflix, comedy and the documentary playbook

Netflix’s documentary catalog includes films about comedians and performers, from the process film Jim & Andy: The Great Beyond, covered in Every Movie Plug’s Jim Carrey Actor Spotlight, to the Savile documentary from the same production company as Open Secret. The Brand film belongs to a different category, closer to an account of a case than a portrait. It is also a reminder that the streamer’s catalog covers everything from scripted war dramas such as The Mosquito Bowl to comedy and sketch projects such as Druski’s Collect and Praise, which arrives in December.

For viewers deciding whether to watch, the practical details are simple: the film is 84 minutes, premieres globally on Friday morning and is available to Netflix subscribers. It deals with allegations of sexual violence, and the court proceedings it covers include testimony from women who say they were assaulted, so viewers should expect difficult material.

What happens next

Three things are worth watching. The first is the reception of the film itself, including whether the women whose testimony led to the convictions have commented on it. The second is Brand’s legal position, since he may have options to appeal and the police assessment of new reports could lead to further proceedings. The third is Netflix’s treatment of Re:Birth, which remains on the service and could come under further pressure.
