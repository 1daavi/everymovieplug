Robert Pattinson Confirms The Batman Part II Is Paused While Matt Reeves Steps Away

By Maccarthy Albin

October 13, 2026

Updated November 1, 2026

DC Studios says director Matt Reeves is stepping away for a family matter, and Pattinson says production is “pausing for a little bit.”

Production on The Batman Part II has been temporarily paused while director Matt Reeves steps away for a family matter, according to a statement from DC Studios reported by Deadline on Monday, October 5. The statement says Reeves has the full support of DC Studios and that the studio looks forward to continuing soon. No details about the matter have been shared, and Reeves has not commented publicly.

Pattinson Speaks at the London Premiere

Robert Pattinson then confirmed the pause himself. According to Just Jared, he told Official Pop Drop at the London premiere of his new film Primetime on Monday, October 12: “Yeah, we're pausing for a little bit, and then we're going to figure it all out.”

The comment is brief. Pattinson did not say why filming stopped, how long the break would be or when the crew would return, and the tone of the remark suggests the production was still working out its next steps at the time.

Where the Schedule Stands

There is no restart date and no estimate of how long the break will last. The film is still scheduled for February 18, 2028, and sources told Deadline it is too early to say whether the pause will affect that date. Reports that Reeves could leave the project have not been substantiated, and the studio’s statement describes a temporary break rather than an exit.

Keeping that distinction in view matters, since speculation tends to grow quickly when a major production halts. The studio used the word “temporarily” and spoke of continuing soon, and nothing in the statement points to a change in the film’s creative leadership. Because the reason is personal, it is also appropriate to leave the matter private and to wait for any further details to come from Reeves or the studio.

Update: Reports of a Return to Set

ARY News reported on October 27 that Pattinson said he would be back on set within days as production resumes after the brief pause. According to that report, DC had previously confirmed that Steven Soderbergh would help complete the final weeks of principal photography, with both filmmakers working with the cast and crew to carry out Reeves’ vision. The report also relays Pattinson’s description of the screenplay as close to complete before the break. Neither the studio nor the production has issued a restart date in the sources cited here, and the release date remains February 18, 2028.

Four Months of Filming Before the Break

Filming had been underway in London since June. According to Wikipedia’s production summary, principal photography began on June 12, 2026, mainly on soundstages at Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden, with location work in Liverpool, Glasgow and London. Reeves, who is writing and directing the sequel to his 2022 film, released a first look at Pattinson in the Batsuit in July.

That means the shoot had been running for nearly four months when the pause was announced. A production of this size typically relies on a tightly coordinated schedule of cast availability, studio bookings and location permits, so even a short interruption requires careful rearranging. How much of the film remained to be shot at the time has not been disclosed.

A Release Date That Keeps Moving

The film’s release date has moved several times, from October 2026 to October 2027 and then to February 2028. The path to the sequel has been long. The 2023 writers’ and actors’ strikes were an early obstacle, and Reeves, who co-wrote the screenplay with Mattson Tomlin, confirmed in June 2025 that the script was finished.

The original The Batman opened in March 2022 and presented a darker, detective-driven take on the character. It also spawned a spin-off series centered on Colin Farrell’s Penguin, which helped keep that corner of Gotham in front of audiences during the long wait for the follow-up.

What a Pause Usually Involves

When a large studio production stops mid-shoot, the effects spread beyond the director’s chair. Sets remain booked, actors hold their calendars for the project and departments such as costumes, visual effects and stunts adjust their timelines. Productions often use a pause to plan the remaining days of filming, and the time lost can sometimes be recovered later through a revised schedule or a shorter post-production period.

That is one reason a studio may hold off on changing a release date. With the film dated for early 2028, there is still room between the end of photography and the premiere for editing, effects work and marketing, although the amount of room depends on when the cameras roll again and how much remains to be filmed.

Who Is in the Cast

Pattinson returns as Bruce Wayne alongside Colin Farrell, Jayme Lawson, Andy Serkis and Jeffrey Wright, who are also back. Scarlett Johansson and Sebastian Stan are among the newcomers. If you are following DC’s upcoming slate, you can also read our explainer on who Clayface is.

The film is part of the DC Studios era overseen by James Gunn and Peter Safran, though Reeves’ Batman stories are set apart from the main DC Universe continuity that Gunn is building. That separation means the sequel’s timeline is not tied directly to other DC releases, which may reduce the knock-on effect of any delay on the wider slate.

What to Watch For

The key points to follow are an official confirmation that filming has resumed, any word on how long the remaining photography will take, and whether the February 18, 2028 date holds. Studios often wait until a production is back on schedule before commenting on release plans, so a change, if there is one, may not be announced right away.

For fans, the practical message is patience. The statements so far emphasize support for Reeves and a desire to continue, and the reports of a return to set suggest the pause was intended to be short. Further details will depend on what DC Studios and Warner Bros. choose to share, and readers should look to official statements rather than unconfirmed reports for any changes to the schedule or the production’s leadership.
