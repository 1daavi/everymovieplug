The Social Reckoning Opens Soft With Around $6 Million

By Maccarthy Albin

October 10, 2026

Sony's Sorkin follow-up is heading for a quiet opening in 2,606 theaters.

The Social Reckoning is off to a quiet start. Aaron Sorkin's follow-up to his The Social Network screenplay earned about $2.1 million on Friday from 2,606 locations, and TheWrap projects an opening weekend of around $6 million. Variety put Friday at $2.15 million, and some outlets have estimated the weekend at closer to $7 million.

Reading the Numbers

Opening-day totals are only the first piece of a weekend forecast. A Friday figure typically includes ticket sales from preview screenings, and analysts then apply an expected pattern for Saturday and Sunday to estimate the three-day total. That is why the reported projections differ. TheWrap’s figure of about $6 million and the higher estimates near $7 million start from nearly identical Friday numbers but make different assumptions about how audiences will turn out over the following two days.

Spread across 2,606 theaters, a $6 million weekend works out to an average of roughly $2,300 per location. That is a modest figure for a wide release, and it shows how thinly the audience is distributed rather than concentrated in a few strong markets.

A Tough Act to Follow

For comparison, The Social Network opened to $22.4 million in 2010, before adjusting for inflation. The new film is released by Sony and co-financed by Alcon, and TheWrap puts the net production spend at $30 million. Jeremy Strong plays Mark Zuckerberg, Jeremy Allen White is among the cast, and Sorkin directs, his fourth film as a director.

The earlier film, directed by David Fincher from Sorkin’s screenplay, won the Oscar for best adapted screenplay and became one of the most closely discussed films of its decade. If the projections hold, The Social Reckoning would open at roughly a quarter of its predecessor’s debut in raw dollars. Because that earlier figure is not adjusted for inflation, the real gap in audience size is likely wider than the headline numbers suggest, though the exact difference depends on the adjustment used.

Sorkin’s own directing record gives some additional background. This is his fourth film behind the camera, following Molly’s Game, The Trial of the Chicago 7 and Being the Ricardos. He is better known as a screenwriter, and the new release asks audiences to connect his name with a story that many viewers first met through someone else’s direction.

Why It May Be Struggling

TheWrap argues that films built around current events and politics have had trouble reaching wide audiences lately, pointing to Digger and the 2022 drama She Said as other examples. It also notes that Sony can absorb the loss, helped by the success of Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

The argument is specific to a certain kind of release: adult-oriented dramas rooted in recent events and public debates. Such films often compete for a smaller group of moviegoers than franchise releases or horror titles, and they depend heavily on strong reviews and word of mouth to build beyond a first weekend. Without the pull of a well-known brand or a high-concept hook, the opening can depend almost entirely on how many people are already interested in the subject.

The Crowded Weekend

The film also arrived alongside other new releases. Variety’s weekend report was headlined around Other Mommy, which it reported earned $12.6 million on opening day, and TheWrap grouped both titles together in describing a slowing box office. A film with a Friday around $2 million sits well below the leading new opener, and the comparison shows how much ground separates a breakout debut from a quiet one on the same weekend.

Sony’s Position

With a net production spend of $30 million and Alcon sharing the financing, the studio’s exposure is lower than it would be on a larger-budget release. TheWrap’s observation that the performance of Spider-Man: Brand New Day cushions the impact reflects a familiar pattern in the business: a single large franchise success can offset the weaker results of smaller, more prestige-oriented titles in the same year.

That said, a film’s theatrical run is not its only source of revenue. Home entertainment, streaming licenses and international sales all contribute over time, and a modest domestic opening does not settle how the film will perform financially in the long run.

What to Watch Next

The weekend figures are early estimates based on Friday, so the final number may move when Monday's actuals arrive.

The next signals will come from the second weekend. The drop between a first and second weekend shows whether audiences are recommending a film to others, and for a drama that depends on conversation and reviews, that figure will say more about its prospects than the opening alone. Until the actuals are published and the following weekend is reported, the available numbers should be read as a snapshot of a quiet debut and not as a final verdict.
