Clayface Earns $3.5 Million-Plus in Thursday Previews

By Maccarthy Albin

October 23, 2026

The R-rated DC Studios film opens against A24's expanding You Can See Everything, with forecasts running from $15 million to $30 million.

Clayface earned an estimated $3.5 million or more in Thursday previews, according to Mezha, which attributes the figure to Deadline and distribution sources. More preview numbers are expected Friday morning.

Reading the Preview Number

Thursday previews are late-evening screenings held the night before a film’s official opening, and their grosses count toward the opening weekend total. They tend to reflect the most eager fans, which is why analysts use them as an early signal but not a final verdict. An estimate of $3.5 million or more places Clayface in solid territory for a character-driven horror-leaning superhero film, although the figure is still preliminary until final numbers arrive.

Forecasts That Do Not Agree

The report puts the weekend forecast at $20 million to $25 million. Other forecasts differ: Box Office Pro projected $25 million to $30 million on October 21, while Box Office Theory forecast $15 million to $23 million the same day.

The spread is wide, with the lowest forecast of $15 million at half the top end of $30 million. That kind of gap is common for films that do not belong to an established franchise formula, because forecasters weigh pre-sales, tracking and social signals differently. The previews narrow the uncertainty somewhat, but the final weekend total will depend heavily on how audiences respond on Friday and Saturday.

How Comparable Films Turned Previews Into Openings

Mezha lists comparable preview-to-opening runs: Birds of Prey took $4 million in previews and opened to $33 million, Other Mommy took $3.6 million and opened to $27.8 million, Smile took $2 million and opened to $22.6 million, and Kraven the Hunter took $2 million and opened to $11 million. Digger took $1.2 million in previews and opened to $8 million (see Digger Opens With $8 Million in Fifth Place).

A simple calculation shows how much the multiplier varies. Kraven the Hunter turned its previews into an opening about 5.5 times as large, Digger about 6.7 times, Other Mommy about 7.7 times, Birds of Prey about 8.3 times and Smile about 11.3 times. Applied to a $3.5 million start, those ratios would produce openings from roughly $19 million to nearly $40 million. These are rough comparisons using the figures reported here, and they should not be read as a prediction, but they help explain why the published forecasts cover such a wide range.

The multiplier usually depends on audience makeup. Films with younger, word-of-mouth-driven audiences, such as horror titles, can see strong weekend growth after previews, while films that rely on a concentrated fan base may be more front-loaded.

The Budget, the Team and the Reviews

Clayface carries a net production cost of $44 million before prints and advertising, per the report, and was shot in the UK. James Watkins directed from a screenplay by Mike Flanagan. Rotten Tomatoes shows a 76% Certified Fresh critics’ score, ahead of The Flash (63%) and Shazam! Fury of the Gods (49%) and close to Birds of Prey (79%), according to the same report.

The $44 million figure is modest for a comic book film, and it does not include the marketing expense that studios typically add. Watkins is known for the thrillers Eden Lake and The Woman in Black, and the project carries an R rating, which gives the filmmakers room to work in a darker register than most superhero releases. Clayface himself is one of Batman’s long-running adversaries, a shape-shifting villain who first appeared in DC Comics in 1940 and has been used by several characters over the decades, a history covered in our explainer Who is Clayface?

The critical score matters because word of mouth is likely to be a factor in the film’s second weekend. A Certified Fresh rating places it ahead of two recent DC theatrical releases mentioned in the report and just behind Birds of Prey, which makes it a comparatively well-reviewed entry for the studio.

A Crowded Weekend Slate

The film faces A24’s You Can See Everything, which opened to $15.6 million last weekend and expands to more than 2,500 screens on Friday, per Box Office Pro. That outlet projects $15 million to $20 million for A24’s film in its second weekend. Street Fighter, which opened to $30.77 million, is projected at $8 million to $13 million in week two (see Street Fighter Wins the Box Office With a $31 Million Opening). Mezha reports about $1 million in previews for Wildwood, and Box Office Theory forecasts $4.5 million to $8.5 million for Wicker.

The competition frames the stakes. If You Can See Everything lands at the upper end of its forecast, it could finish close to the lower end of Clayface’s range, which makes the top spot a real contest. Whether the two films compete for the same audience is not addressed in the sources cited here, and the final ranking will depend on how each performs on Saturday as well as Friday. A narrow margin between them would not be unusual, since the forecasts for both overlap in the mid-teens.

The weekend also shows how the fall calendar is filling up. With a video game adaptation in its second week, an A24 title expanding and several new releases in smaller lanes, Clayface is one of many titles competing for the same moviegoers.

What to Watch as the Weekend Unfolds

The next data point is Friday’s updated preview figure, followed by daily estimates and Sunday’s projected totals. Two things will signal how the weekend is going: whether Friday business is closer to the front-loaded or the word-of-mouth models noted above, and how Saturday compares. Audience scores and exit polling, when they are published, add further context.

For DC Studios, the result will also be read against the film’s relatively low cost. A moderate opening could still be viewed as a success if the film holds well, while a soft start would put more weight on international performance and later release windows. The sources used here do not report international figures.
