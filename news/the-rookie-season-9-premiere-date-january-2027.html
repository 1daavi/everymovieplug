The Rookie Season 9 Sets January 6 Premiere, With Spinoff North

By Maccarthy Albin

October 11, 2026

The two-part premiere will be followed by the debut of The Rookie: North starring Jay Ellis.

ABC’s police drama is getting a companion, and the network has told viewers exactly when to find both. The Rookie will return for its ninth season on Wednesday, January 6, 2027, at 9/8c on ABC. Nathan Fillion announced the date at New York Comic Con on Sunday. It will be followed at 10/9c by the series premiere of the spinoff The Rookie: North, headlined by Jay Ellis, Deadline reports. Both shows stream on Hulu the next day.

Picking Up From a Cliffhanger

The Season 9 premiere is a two-parter. A sizzle reel shown at the panel suggested the season picks up with John Nolan and the team on a mission to rescue LAPD officers Tim Bradford (Eric Winter) and Lucy Chen (Melissa O’Neil), who were kidnapped in the Season 8 finale cliffhanger. That finale aired on May 4.

Showrunner Alexi Hawley, who confirmed that production on Season 9 began on July 21, described the premiere script as fun, romantic and emotional with a lot of action. For a series that has long balanced procedural cases with workplace relationships, that mix is a familiar one, and opening with a rescue story would let the writers address the cliffhanger right away.

New Faces at the Panel

The panel also announced guest stars. Troian Bellisario, who directed the Season 8 episode “The Thinker,” will play Nolan’s new rookie, Stephanie Monroe, a single mom of two who is rebuilding her life. The casting gives the show a new trainee for Nolan, who started the series as the oldest rookie in the department and now guides others through the same difficult first year.

Andy Allo will guest star in North as Brooke, a first-year ER resident who forms a connection with Ellis’s rookie police officer, Give Me My Remote reports.

Meet The Rookie: North

The spinoff follows Alex Holland, played by Ellis, a man who joins the Pierce County Police Department after a violent home invasion changes the direction of his life. He starts over in a new career, much as Nolan did in the original series. ABC ordered North to series in May, making it the network’s first new scripted series order for the 2026–27 season, and the first season is set to run for ten episodes. Ellis is best known for the HBO comedy Insecure.

The ensemble includes Chris Sullivan, Karen Fukuhara, Froy Gutierrez, Janet Montgomery, Mya Lowe and Malik Watson. Fillion is an executive producer on both series, and he appeared as Nolan in the spinoff’s pilot, which sets up the connection between the two shows.

Two Cities, Two Looks

North is set in the Pacific Northwest and shoots in Vancouver, while The Rookie is set and filmed in Los Angeles. Hawley has said that the change of setting will visually make the spinoff feel very different, with urban areas and isolated rural communities where backup can be hard to reach. He has also said that crossovers will happen, though the distance between the two production bases makes them harder to arrange. Reports suggest a small number of crossover episodes each season.

The move to Wednesdays is the show’s eighth time-period change, Deadline notes, and the show will run in a block with its spinoff. Pairing the two series back to back is the simplest way to make crossovers feel like a natural part of the schedule rather than a special event.

A Premise Built on Starting Over

The original series follows John Nolan, played by Fillion, a man who moves to Los Angeles after a life change and becomes the oldest rookie in the LAPD. The idea of a late-career fresh start gave the show its identity, and it is the idea that the franchise is now repeating in other settings. Holland’s story in North echoes it in a new way, since his decision to join the police comes after a traumatic event rather than a midlife restlessness. The shared theme gives the two series a natural connection without requiring them to tell the same stories.

What the Schedule Means for Viewers

The January 6 date places the show in the midseason window that ABC has used for the series in recent years, and the CBR report on the announcement describes a continuing trend of winter returns. For viewers, the practical details are simple. The Rookie airs first at 9/8c, North follows at 10/9c, and both are available on Hulu the day after they air. Anyone who needs to catch up before January can find the earlier seasons on streaming, and the two-part premiere should give new viewers a clear point of entry into the ninth season. With the ten-episode order for North, the spinoff’s first season is expected to be a compact one.

The Franchise Gets a Second Try

The Rookie universe has been here before. The Rookie: Feds, which starred Niecy Nash-Betts as the oldest rookie at the FBI academy, aired on ABC in the 2022–23 season and was canceled after one season. North is the second spinoff, and its creators have positioned it as a standalone show with its own identity rather than a branch of the original. The strategy is a response to what worked for the flagship, which launched in 2018 and has outlasted most network dramas by centering on a likable lead, a mix of humor and danger, and a premise that speaks to second chances.

That longevity matters. A ninth season is a notable milestone for a network drama, and the decision to build a second show around the same premise reflects confidence in the format. Whether viewers will follow the franchise to a new setting is the main question heading into January.
