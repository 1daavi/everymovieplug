Johnny Depp's Scrooge Joins Venmo, and He Pays Fans Back One Cent

By Maccarthy Albin

October 23, 2026

A promotional Venmo account for Ebenezer Scrooge is live, with a catch: generosity has its limits.

Ebenezer Scrooge now has a Venmo account. Paramount's promotional profile for Ebenezer: A Christmas Carol launched this week, inviting fans to send the miser payment requests ahead of the film's November 13 release, according to Cosmic Book News and ComingSoon.

How the Joke Works

The account lives at venmo.com/u/EBENEZERmovie, per Cosmic Book News, and was announced by the film's official X account. In a 29-second launch video, a fan requests $500 labeled “student loans.” Scrooge pays back one cent with the note “Generosity has its limits,” according to ComingSoon and Cosmic Book News.

The gag depends on a simple reversal. Venmo is an app people use to send money to friends, and a profile for one of literature’s most famous misers invites exactly the kind of request he would be expected to refuse. By returning a single cent, the character turns a refusal into a small payment, which makes the stunt feel like a response from Scrooge himself and not just a promotional post. The launch video presents the exchange in a way that is easy to understand in under half a minute, which suits social media sharing.

Is It Real? What the Reports Say

ComingSoon reports that fans can send requests but that Scrooge answers with a penny. Variety’s headline asked whether the account was real and described a profile that “looks to be accepting money but not dishing it out.”

The two accounts of the stunt differ in emphasis. ComingSoon focuses on what happens when a fan sends a request, namely a penny in return, while Variety’s coverage raised the question of whether the profile was genuine at all and whether it only collected money. The available reports do not settle every detail of how the account operates behind the scenes, so the safest reading is that it is a promotional profile tied to the film, launched through the film’s official X account, with its exact mechanics described differently by different outlets.

The Film and Its Cast

Ebenezer: A Christmas Carol is a Paramount Pictures release directed by Ti West from a screenplay by Nathaniel Halpern. Johnny Depp plays Scrooge, with Rupert Grint, Tramell Tillman, Andrea Riseborough, Sam Claflin, Daisy Ridley, Arthur Conti, Ellie Bamber, Henry Lloyd-Hughes, Charlie Murphy and Ian McKellen, per ComingSoon.

The story comes from Charles Dickens’s 1843 novella A Christmas Carol, in which a bitter, wealthy man is visited by ghosts who show him the consequences of his selfishness. It is one of the most frequently adapted stories in English literature, so each new version has to find its own angle. West is best known for horror films, which makes his involvement in a Dickens adaptation notable, though the reports used here do not describe the tone of the finished film.

The cast list is long, combining actors known from fantasy franchises, prestige drama and television. How their roles map onto Dickens’s characters is not specified in the cited coverage, so those details remain for the film itself.

Why Scrooge Still Works as a Brand

Dickens’s story introduced elements that remain widely recognized, including the ghost of Jacob Marley, the three spirits of Christmas past, present and yet to come, and the clerk Bob Cratchit. Scrooge himself has become a common shorthand for stinginess, and the name is used in everyday language far beyond the book. That cultural footprint gives a studio a powerful advantage in marketing: audiences do not need to be introduced to the character, so a short video can deliver its joke immediately. It also explains why a stunt centered on money fits so naturally with a film built on a story about the meaning of wealth.

Marketing Built on a Famous Character

The Venmo account fits into a familiar approach to film promotion, in which a studio gives a character a presence on a platform that audiences already use. Scrooge is especially suited to a payments app because his defining trait is his relationship with money. A joke about a refusal to give, expressed through the exact tool people use to give, needs no explanation for viewers who know the character, and the one-cent response adds a punchline that is easy to share.

Timing Before Release

The film opens in theaters on November 13, about three weeks after the Venmo account launched.

That gap gives the campaign time to circulate before opening weekend. A stunt that launches three weeks ahead of a release can build awareness gradually, A release in mid-November also places the film at the start of the holiday season, which suits a story associated with Christmas.
