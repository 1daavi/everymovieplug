Fallout 5: What Bethesda Has Confirmed So Far

By Maccarthy Albin

October 24, 2026

The game is in preproduction, with remasters and an Obsidian spin-off arriving first.

Fallout 5 is in preproduction at Bethesda Game Studios, and no release date or window has been announced. Bethesda confirmed the status in a July 17 studio update covered by Game Informer, which says the game “remains our long-range destination.”

For fans who have waited since the 2015 release of Fallout 4 for word on the next main entry, the update provides a formal answer: the game exists as a project, but it is at the earliest stage of development and sits behind other work at the studio. The same announcement also laid out a wider roadmap for the franchise, which gives a clearer picture of what players can expect in the meantime.

Engine and Priorities: Where Fallout 5 Sits in the Queue

Game Informer reports that Fallout 5 is being built on Creation Engine 3, which will also power The Elder Scrolls VI. Bethesda calls The Elder Scrolls VI “our primary development focus today,” and Game Informer says Fallout 5 comes after it. The Movie Blog, citing a Collider report, says the game is unlikely to release before 2030.

The shared technology matters for two reasons. A common engine lets the studio carry tools and improvements from one project to the next, and it suggests that work done to support The Elder Scrolls VI will also benefit the later game. It also means the two titles are tied together in terms of schedule, since the engine has to be in a stable state before a second large game can fully build on it. The 2030 estimate is a report rather than an announcement, and Bethesda itself has not named a year.

Preproduction is the phase in which a studio defines a game's concept, tests ideas and plans its tools before the full team begins building content. For a Bethesda title, which typically involves a large open world, many quests and extensive systems, that stage can run for a considerable time, and a game in preproduction is generally years from release.

What Arrives Before the Next Mainline Game

Other Fallout projects confirmed in the update, per Game Informer: remasters of Fallout 3 and Fallout: New Vegas with no release windows, a new Fallout game from Obsidian, a Fallout 76 expansion called Raven Rock in 2027 and new seasons for Fallout Shelter. Amazon is developing an unscripted TV adaptation of the mobile game.

The list shows a franchise operating on several fronts at once. Fallout 3 arrived in 2008 under Bethesda, and Fallout: New Vegas, developed by Obsidian, followed in 2010, so the remasters will bring two of the series' most discussed entries to a newer audience. The involvement of Obsidian in a fresh game is a notable detail given the studio's association with New Vegas. Meanwhile, Fallout 76, which launched in 2018, continues to receive content, and the Raven Rock expansion is scheduled for 2027.

The unscripted adaptation of Fallout Shelter is a separate branch of the franchise's reach into television. It follows the Amazon live-action series that debuted in 2024, which helped bring new attention to the games, and shows the company exploring other formats built on the same property.

The Xbox Question Remains Open

Windows Central reports that Todd Howard said it was too early to say whether Fallout 5 or The Elder Scrolls VI would be Xbox exclusive.

The question carries weight because Microsoft acquired Bethesda's parent company, ZeniMax Media, in 2021, and players have since watched how the company handles platform availability for the studio's games. Howard's answer neither confirms nor rules out exclusivity, and with the games years away, any decision is likely to come much later in their development.

Anniversary Plans and a Skipped Broadcast

Bethesda said there will be no Fallout Day broadcast this year. The franchise’s 30th anniversary will be marked in 2027 with a live event in Washington, D.C., according to Game Informer.

The decision to skip the annual broadcast shifts attention to 2027, when the series reaches three decades since its first game appeared in 1997. A live event in the nation's capital fits the setting of Fallout 3, which takes place in the ruins of the Washington area, and it gives the studio a public occasion to mark the milestone. For a series built on a retro-futuristic vision of American history, a gathering in that city offers a fitting backdrop for celebrating three decades of games, and it gives fans a defined occasion to look toward after a year without the usual broadcast.

How to Read the Timeline

Taken together, the confirmed information points to a sequence in which The Elder Scrolls VI comes first, Fallout 5 follows, and a series of remasters, expansions and spin-offs fill the gap. Fans should treat the 2030 estimate as a reported expectation rather than a promise, and watch for further updates from Bethesda as the studio moves through its current projects.
