Grok Imagine Releases Episode 1 of Its AI-Made Odyssey Series

By Maccarthy Albin

October 25, 2026

The roughly seven-minute episode, made with Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker, follows Elon Musk’s July promise of an AI Odyssey film.

Grok Imagine, xAI’s AI video generator, has released Episode 1 of an Odyssey series made in collaboration with Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker. The episode runs a little over seven minutes and covers the Greeks’ arrival at Troy, according to Mashable.

Watch Episode 1 of our Odyssey series made with Grok Imagine. Creative collaboration with @WonderStudiosX and director Dev Walker @glumlot_

— Grok Imagine (@imagine) October 23, 2026

The release is the latest step in an effort by xAI to show what its generative video tools can do with a well-known story, and it arrives at a moment when Homer's epic is already in the public eye because of a major theatrical adaptation. The two projects could hardly differ more in how they were made, which is part of why the episode has drawn attention.

How the Episode Reached Viewers

Grok Imagine posted the episode on X late Friday afternoon, October 23, and offered to share a full breakdown of how it was made if viewers ask. Wonder Studios posted, “Homer didn’t have Grok… we do.” Mashable describes the project as the first of several planned episodes.

Releasing a short episode on a social platform and inviting questions about the process is a deliberate strategy. It treats the production method as part of the pitch, and it signals that the creators expect viewers to be curious about how a series can be assembled with generative tools. The framing as the first of several episodes also means the release should be read as a starting point, with later installments likely to show how the approach develops.

A Promise Made in July

The release follows a promise Elon Musk made in July that Grok Imagine would produce a full-length Odyssey movie before the end of the year that was “historically accurate and true to the art of Homer,” per Mashable. Musk has also criticized Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey, mainly over historical accuracy and casting, and said in February that Nolan had “lost his integrity.”

The context matters for understanding the project. Musk's July promise and his criticism of Nolan's film place the two projects in direct comparison, and the seven-minute episode is a modest first step toward a promise that referred to a full-length movie. Whether the series will grow into a feature-length work has not been announced, and Mashable's description of several planned episodes suggests the format may be episodic rather than a single film.

The Technology Behind the Series

xAI, the company founded by Musk in 2023, is also the developer of the Grok chatbot, and Grok Imagine extends the same family of tools to images and video. Generative video systems typically produce short clips from text prompts, so a longer piece such as this episode is assembled from many individual shots that must be edited together and kept consistent in look and character design. That process explains why collaborators such as a studio and a director are involved: the system generates material, but people decide what to prompt, what to keep and how to cut it into a story.

What Reviewers Noticed on Screen

Mashable’s review of the episode points to common generative AI flaws, including unnatural teeth, odd hair texture and smudgy skin, and calls the writing weak and the characters hard to tell apart. It notes that Odysseus is the only named character and that every character is white.

These observations reflect issues that have followed AI video since the technology emerged. Fine details such as teeth, hair and skin are difficult for generative systems to render consistently, and keeping a character recognizable from shot to shot is another persistent challenge. The comment that only Odysseus is named points to a script that gives little individual identity to the other figures, which can make a story harder to follow. The observation about the cast concerns the characters shown on screen and comes from the review itself.

The $100,000 Contest

Grok Imagine previously ran a contest with a $100,000 prize for the best Odyssey depiction made on its platform. Digital Camera World reports that the winner, Nem Perez, submitted a five-minute entry centered on Nausicaa and the Phaeacians, and that the entry showed visible anomalies, including a cyclops with three eyes, per Futurism.

Nausicaa is the Phaeacian princess who helps the shipwrecked Odysseus in the epic, so the winning entry drew on one of the poem's more tender episodes. The reported three-eyed cyclops is a notable error, since the cyclops of the story is defined by having a single eye. Details like this illustrate the gap between generating striking images and reproducing the specifics of a text, and they are the kind of flaw that observers have used to assess how far the technology has come.

Set Against Nolan's Blockbuster

Nolan’s The Odyssey opened to $124.5 million in July and had grossed more than $1.7 billion worldwide, according to Mashable. The film arrives on digital platforms on November 17. Nolan has said that the idea of generative AI replacing human creativity is nonsense, per the same report.

The contrast in scale is stark. A film that has earned more than $1.7 billion worldwide reflects an enormous audience response, while a seven-minute episode posted on a social platform operates at a very different level of production and reach. The two works nevertheless share source material, and their coexistence has turned the ancient epic into a debate about the role of artificial intelligence in storytelling. Nolan's stated position and Musk's promotion of the AI project stand on opposite sides of that debate.

Why the Odyssey Keeps Returning

Homer's poem, which tells of Odysseus's long journey home after the Trojan War, has been retold in countless forms for centuries. Its mix of monsters, gods, voyages and homecoming offers material suited to visual treatment, which is why filmmakers and technologists alike return to it. The episode's focus on the Greeks' arrival at Troy places it at the start of the larger saga, since the war at Troy precedes the voyage that gives the poem its name.

As further episodes appear, viewers will be able to judge whether the series addresses the issues reviewers raised about consistency, writing and character, and whether the project moves toward the full-length film Musk described in July. For now, the first episode stands as an early and openly experimental entry in the growing field of AI-generated storytelling.
