A short guide

How to choose a movie tonight

  1. 1

    Start with the mood, not the title.

    Decide what kind of evening you want before you scroll. A tense thriller and a comfort rewatch are different decisions, and choosing the feeling first makes the shortlist much smaller.

  2. 2

    Be honest about the runtime.

    A two-hour film and a three-hour epic are different commitments. If it is a weeknight, check the length first so you are not deciding at 11 p.m. whether to stop.

  3. 3

    Read two kinds of scores.

    Critics and audiences often disagree. Look at one critic-based score and one audience-based score, then skim a review or two to see why they differ. A divisive film can be exactly what you want.

  4. 4

    Check where it is streaming before you commit.

    Catalogs change by country and by month. Open the title page to see where it is available in your region, and whether it is included with a subscription or has to be rented.

  5. 5

    Give it a fair test, then move on.

    If a film has not hooked you after the first twenty minutes, it is fine to switch. Life is short, and the watchlist is long.

Know what you are looking at

Ratings, explained

Every film page on Every Movie Plug can show several scores. They measure different things, so it helps to know what each one means.

TMDB user score

The average of ratings from registered TMDB members, out of 10. It reflects what everyday viewers think, and newer films can swing a lot until enough people have voted.

IMDb rating

A weighted average of votes from IMDb users on a scale of 1 to 10. It is useful for comparing films across decades because so many people have voted on the classics.

Rotten Tomatoes

The Tomatometer is the percentage of critic reviews that are positive, not an average grade. A film at 80% is liked by most critics, but that does not mean they all gave it 8 out of 10. The Audience Score is a separate number.

Metacritic

The Metascore converts critic reviews to a 0 to 100 scale and averages them with extra weight for some outlets. It is a better guide to how strongly critics liked a film than a simple fresh-or-rotten split.

CinemaScore

A grade from A+ to F based on polling of opening-weekend moviegoers. It captures first-night audience reaction, which is why a film can have mixed reviews but a strong grade, or the reverse. We covered one such gap in our report on Other Mommy and its D+ CinemaScore.

Questions

Frequently asked

How are the trending movies chosen?+

The weekly list comes from TMDB's trending chart, which is based on activity on TMDB. It is a good snapshot of what people are looking up and rating right now, not a ranking of box office or streaming hours.

Can I watch movies on Every Movie Plug?+

No. We do not host or stream films. Title pages show where a film is legally available, using streaming data from JustWatch, so you can find it on the service you already use.

Where do the ratings come from?+

TMDB provides the user score and listings, and critic and audience scores from sources such as IMDb, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic appear on title pages when they are available.

How often is this page updated?+

The trending list refreshes through the week. News is published as stories develop, and we update an existing story when there is a new development instead of repeating it.

How do I report a mistake or suggest a topic?+

Email contact@everymovieplug.com or message us on X at @everymovieplug. We read everything and correct errors we confirm.
